# عُصْبَة — A Band of Strong Men

**عُصْبَة** denotes a strong band or group — and the word is far more specific than a general "group."

---

## Who It Covers

!!! important "Men Only, and of a Particular Age"
    **عُصْبَة** is used **only for men**, and specifically for men roughly between **10 and 40** years of age — the young, healthy, physically capable.

    Past forty, a man is no longer at that peak of physical labour. And although ten sounds like a child to modern ears, in those times boys of eleven and twelve were already working.

---

## Plural and Pattern

**عُصْبَة → عُصَب**, on the pattern **فُعْلَة → فُعَل**:

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| **عُصْبَة** | **عُصَب** |
| **غُرْفَة** (room) | **غُرَف** |
| **ظُلْمَة** (darkness) | **ظُلَم** |

See [[fuala-pattern]].

## Examples from the Quran

> **إِذْ قَالُوا لَيُوسُفُ وَأَخُوهُ أَحَبُّ إِلَىٰ أَبِينَا مِنَّا وَنَحْنُ عُصْبَةٌ** (Yūsuf 12:8)
>
> *"…while we are a strong band."*

!!! info "The Social Logic Behind the Brothers' Complaint"
    The word is doing real work in the argument. Grown sons were **assets**: they could farm, they could shepherd, they multiplied the household's material worth. Young children were, in that reckoning, a **liability** — to be fed and raised before returning anything.

    So the brothers' grievance has an internal logic: *we* are ten, grown, strong and productive; Yūsuf and his brother are two small children. On a purely material accounting, our father should love **us** more.

    Two qualifications were stressed in class: this materialistic mindset has been present in **every** age — *"we are seeing the height of materialism now"* — and it emphatically does **not** describe Yaʿqūb عليه السلام. It describes the brothers' assumptions, which is exactly what leads them to call him **فِي ضَلَالٍ مُبِينٍ** (see [[root-dalla]]).
