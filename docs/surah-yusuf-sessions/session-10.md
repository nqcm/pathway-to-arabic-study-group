# Surah Yusuf — Study Session 10

---

## Overview

The main topics covered in this session are:

- **Recap of عَطْف البَيَان** — the two distinguishing points from [Session 9](session-9.md), with a refinement of how to think about "concern"
- **عَطْف البَيَان and بَدَل كُلّ مِن كُلّ are interchangeable ~95% of the time** — every ʿaṭf al-bayān can be a badal kull min kull, but not the other way round
- **The litmus test for بَدَل** — the ʿāmil is repeated "under the hood", so each element must survive on its own; the **edge cases** where the test fails and only **عَطْف البَيَان** is possible
- Edge cases: **زَيْنَبُ جَاءَنِي أَخُوهَا بِلَالٌ**, a **munādā followed by a word with ال**, and Abū Ṭālib's couplet **أَيَا أَخَوَيْنَا عَبْدَ شَمْسٍ وَنَوْفَلًا**
- **Quranic examples of عَطْف البَيَان** — كَفَّارَةٌ طَعَامُ مَسَاكِينَ، شَجَرَةٍ مُبَارَكَةٍ زَيْتُونَةٍ، مَاءٍ صَدِيدٍ
- **Back to the book — Āyah 8**: Dr. Abdur Raheem's recommended study method; al-Qurṭubī's explanation of **ضَلَال** (إِيثَار، اِسْتِوَاء، اِنْتِسَاب)
- **حَبَّ vs أَحَبَّ** — Form I verb rarely used, Form IV maṣdar rarely used; **حُبًّا** as mafʿūl muṭlaq with a Form IV verb
- **لَام الاِبتِدَاء** and the ḥadīth **لَغَدْوَةٌ فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ أَوْ رَوْحَةٌ**
- **اللَّام المُزَحلَقَة** — its movement to the khabar, to a delayed ism, and onto **ضَمِير الفَصل**; **لَهْو vs لَعِب**; **الحَيَوَان**
- **Ibn Mālik's line** وَبَعْدَ ذَاتِ الكَسْرِ تَصْحَبُ الخَبَرْ — the lām only after **إِنَّ**, never after **أَنَّ**; **the four levels of emphasis**

## 1. Recap — عَطْف النَّسَق and عَطْف البَيَان

### 1.1 Two Kinds of عَطْف

**عَطْف** means *conjunction* — something inserted into the sentence to join. There are two kinds:

| Type | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **عَطْف النَّسَق** | The familiar conjunction with a **حَرْف عَطْف** — **وَ**, **ثُمَّ**, **فَ** | **جَاءَ بِلَالٌ وَمُحَمَّدٌ** · **اِشْتَرَيْتُ كِتَابًا وَقَلَمًا** |
| **عَطْف البَيَان** | The conjunction of **explanation** (**بَيَان**) — it comes to explain the first word | **قَابَلْتُ إِلِيزَابِيث مَلِكَةَ بِرِيطَانِيَا** |

!!! note "Terminology"
    In a badal construction the two elements are the **بَدَل** (the second) and the **مُبْدَل مِنْه** (the first — the word for which the badal is brought). The teacher used "first element" and "second element" loosely for convenience.

### 1.2 Point One — The Second Element Is Better Known or Clearer

!!! example "Better Known"
    - **قَابَلْتُ إِلِيزَابِيث مَلِكَةَ بِرِيطَانِيَا** — thousands of women are named Elizabeth; **مَلِكَة بِرِيطَانِيَا** makes clear which one.
    - **أَقْسَمَ بِاللَّهِ أَبُو حَفْصٍ عُمَرُ** — a classic example found everywhere in the grammar books. **أَبُو حَفْص** is the kunyah of ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb رضي الله عنه, but it is not well known; **عُمَر** clarifies who is meant.

!!! example "Clearer — هَلْ عِنْدَكَ مُنْتَقِلٌ أَيْ جَوَّالٌ؟"
    In some places (such as the UAE) **مُنْتَقِل** is the familiar word for a mobile phone; in others (such as Saudi Arabia) **جَوَّال** is. If you are in Saudi Arabia and say **مُنْتَقِل**, you add **أَيْ جَوَّالٌ** to make it clear. **جَوَّال** here is **عَطْف البَيَان**, and **أَيْ** is **أَيْ التَّفسِيرِيَّة**.

This is **not** the case for badal. In **أَيْنَ أَخُوكَ هَاشِمٌ**, Hāshim is not necessarily better known than "your brother" — in badal, both elements are at roughly the same level of clarity.

### 1.3 Point Two — Where the Concern Lies (Refined)

In **أَيْنَ أَخُوكَ هَاشِمٌ**, the person I am interested in is **Hāshim**. If you have more than one brother, it is Hāshim I want; his being your brother is not the primary concern. The concern is the **second** element — that is **بَدَل**.

!!! tip "A Better Way to Hold This Rule"
    Some books define it as: *in ʿaṭf al-bayān the first element is the concern.* But not all the examples adhere to that. The teacher prefers to think of it this way:

    - **In بَدَل, the second element is the concern.**
    - **In عَطْف البَيَان, that might not be the case** — sometimes the first element feels like the greater concern, sometimes there is no difference in concern between the two.

!!! note "Context Changes Things"
    These are subtle differences and depend heavily on context. If I want to meet *one of your brothers*, his being Hāshim may not be the primary emphasis. But taken as an individual sentence in a vacuum, **أَيْنَ أَخُوكَ هَاشِمٌ** is **بَدَل**, not ʿaṭf al-bayān, because the primary concern is the person Hāshim.

### 1.4 Quick Recap of the Badal Types

| Type | Example | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **بَدَل كُلّ مِن كُلّ** | **جَاءَ الأُسْتَاذُ أَحْمَدُ** · **أَخُوكَ هَاشِمٌ** | Identical — the same person |
| **بَدَل بَعْض مِن كُلّ** | **أَكَلْتُ الدَّجَاجَةَ نِصْفَهَا** — *I ate the chicken, half of it* | Half a chicken is not the chicken |
| **بَدَل اِشْتِمَال** | **أَعْجَبَنِي الكِتَابُ لُغَتُهُ** — *The book pleased me, its language* | The language is not the book |

See [Badal](../topics/badal.md).

## 2. عَطْف البَيَان and بَدَل كُلّ مِن كُلّ Are Interchangeable

!!! important "The New Point"
    **عَطْف البَيَان** and **بَدَل كُلّ مِن كُلّ** are **interchangeable most of the time** — roughly **95%** of the time.

    - **Any ʿaṭf al-bayān can be a بَدَل كُلّ مِن كُلّ** — except in some **edge cases**.
    - **Not the other way round**: not every badal can be ʿaṭf al-bayān.

!!! example "Elizabeth Again"
    **قَابَلْتُ إِلِيزَابِيث مَلِكَةَ بِرِيطَانِيَا** was called ʿaṭf al-bayān because **مَلِكَة بِرِيطَانِيَا** is better known. But if you call it a **badal**, that is **100% correct**. Which badal? Obviously **بَدَل كُلّ مِن كُلّ** — you cannot call it badal ishtimāl or badal baʿḍ min kull.

So the question becomes: **what are the edge cases** where only ʿaṭf al-bayān is possible? The teacher worked through examples first and then established the rule.

## 3. The Litmus Test for بَدَل

### 3.1 Under the Hood, a Badal Is Two Sentences

!!! example "جَاءَنِي أَخُوهَا بِلَالٌ"
    Start simple: **جَاءَنِي أَخُوهَا** — *Her brother came to me.*

    - **جَاءَ** — the **فِعل**
    - **ي** (with **نُون الوِقَايَة**) — **مَفْعُول بِه**
    - **أَخُو** — **فَاعِل**, and **هَا** — **مُضَاف إِلَيه**

    Now add **بِلَالٌ**: **جَاءَنِي أَخُوهَا بِلَالٌ** — *Her brother Bilāl came to me.* **بِلَال** is a **بَدَل** from **أَخُو**. And if, from the context, Bilāl is better known, it could also be **عَطْف البَيَان** — the two are interchangeable here.

!!! important "The ʿĀmil Is Repeated in Badal"
    Think of a badal as **two sentences under the hood**:

    > **جَاءَ أَخُوكَ** + **جَاءَ بِلَالٌ**

    The **عَامِل** (here **جَاءَ**, which makes **أَخُو** marfūʿ as fāʿil and **بِلَال** marfūʿ too) is **implicitly repeated** for the badal, even though we do not say **جَاءَ** a second time.

    **In عَطْف البَيَان the ʿāmil is not repeated.**

!!! tip "The Test"
    Take the sentence and **remove one element at a time**. If **both** resulting sentences are still correct — in grammar and in meaning — it can be a **true badal**. If either one breaks, it **cannot** be badal; it is **عَطْف البَيَان**.

!!! example "Running the Test on جَاءَنِي أَخُوكَ بِلَالٌ"
    | Remove | Result | Correct? |
    |---|---|---|
    | **بِلَالٌ** | **جَاءَنِي أَخُوكَ** | Yes |
    | **أَخُوكَ** | **جَاءَنِي بِلَالٌ** | Yes |

    Both pass → it can be **بَدَل**.

### 3.2 Edge Case 1 — The Meaning Breaks: زَيْنَبُ جَاءَنِي أَخُوهَا بِلَالٌ

Change the structure: **زَيْنَبُ جَاءَنِي أَخُوهَا بِلَالٌ** — *Zaynab: her brother Bilāl came to me.* The analysis of **أَخُوهَا بِلَالٌ** is the same as before.

| Remove | Result | Correct? |
|---|---|---|
| **بِلَالٌ** | **زَيْنَبُ جَاءَنِي أَخُوهَا** — *Zaynab, her brother came to me* | Yes |
| **أَخُوهَا** | **زَيْنَبُ جَاءَنِي بِلَالٌ** | **No** — it does not make sense |

!!! important "Verdict"
    The litmus test fails. Had **زَيْنَب** not been there, it could have been badal; in this formation of the sentence it **cannot** be badal. It is **100% عَطْف البَيَان**.

    (Removing **أَخُوهَا** also removes the **هَا** that ties the rest of the sentence back to **زَيْنَب** — which is why the sentence collapses.)

### 3.3 Edge Case 2 — The Grammar Breaks: A Munādā Followed by ال

#### Names That Were Adjectives

!!! info "العَبَّاس"
    **عَبَّاس** comes from **عَبَسَ / يَعْبِسُ** — *to frown*. **عَبَّاس** is a **مُبَالَغَة** form, a name given to the **lion** because it frowns a lot. From the same root, **عَبُوس** is a title given to the Day of Judgement, because it is a day that will make people frown.

    **عَبَّاس** is a proper name — but not a **جَامِد** one. It is a **صِفَة**, a characteristic that has been made into a proper name.

!!! note "Adjective-Names Can Take ال"
    Proper names that began life as adjectives can sometimes take **ال** and be used adjectivally: **العَبَّاس**, **الحَسَن**, **الحُسَيْن**. **حَسَن** and **حُسَيْن** are proper names, but **الحَسَن** and **الحُسَيْن** can be adjectives used for someone else.

    You **cannot** put **ال** in front of just any proper name to turn it into an adjective — we restrict ourselves to the ones the Arabs actually used this way.

#### The Test

!!! example "يَا بِلَالُ العَبَّاس"
    **يَا** — **حَرْف نِدَاء**; **بِلَال** — **مُنَادَى**; **العَبَّاس** — looks like a **بَدَل** from Bilāl (kunyahs, titles, and adjectives like this are typically badals).

    | Remove | Result | Correct? |
    |---|---|---|
    | **العَبَّاس** | **يَا بِلَالُ** | Yes |
    | **بِلَال** | **يَا العَبَّاس** | **No** |

    Why? When **يَا** comes before anything beginning with **ال**, you must insert **أَيُّهَا**: **يَا أَيُّهَا الإِنسَانُ** — never **يَا الإِنسَانُ**. So **يَا العَبَّاس** is ungrammatical; it would have to be **يَا أَيُّهَا العَبَّاس**.

    This time it is not the **meaning** that breaks but the **grammar**. The test fails → **عَطْف البَيَان**, not badal. See [Munada](../topics/munada-nida.md).

!!! note "Why This Is an Edge Case"
    Constructions like these — this kind of munādā — do not come up all the time. That is why ʿaṭf al-bayān and badal kull min kull are interchangeable ~95% of the time.

### 3.4 Edge Case 3 — Abū Ṭālib's Couplet

> **أَيَا أَخَوَيْنَا عَبْدَ شَمْسٍ وَنَوْفَلًا** ⁂ **أُعِيذُكُمَا بِاللَّهِ أَنْ تُحْدِثَا حَرْبَا**
>
> *"O our two brothers, ʿAbd Shams and Nawfal — I commit you both to Allah's protection from starting a new war."*

!!! info "Background and Vocabulary"
    - **عَبْد شَمْس** and **نَوْفَل** were two tribes. The teacher was not 100% sure of the exact lineage — ʿAbd Shams was either a brother of **Hāshim** (the great-grandfather of the Prophet ﷺ) or his uncle. Nawfal was another of the tribes; both were brothers.
    - **أَعَاذَ / يُعِيذُ** — *to place someone in Allah's protection*. Compare **أَعُوذُ بِاللَّهِ مِنَ الشَّيْطَانِ الرَّجِيمِ** — *I take refuge with Allah from the rejected Shayṭān*. **أُعِيذُكُمَا بِاللَّهِ** — *I place you both in Allah's protection*.
    - **أَنْ تُحْدِثَا حَرْبَا** — *that you start (bring about) something new — a new war*.

!!! example "Parsing the First Line"
    - **أَخَوَيْنَا** — munādā, **مَنصُوب** because it is **مُضَاف** (to **نَا**)
    - **عَبْدَ شَمْسٍ** — badal or ʿaṭf al-bayān? We have to figure it out. It is manṣūb.
    - **نَوْفَلًا** — joined by **وَ**, also manṣūb

!!! question "A Student's Question — And for a One-Word Name Like Bilāl?"
    When **يَا** comes before a single-word name, **بِلَالٌ** becomes **بِلَالُ** — it **drops its tanwīn** but does **not** become manṣūb. Only the **mudāf–mudāf ilayh** munādā becomes manṣūb.

#### The Test

| Element | Remove the first element | Correct? |
|---|---|---|
| **عَبْدَ شَمْسٍ** | **يَا عَبْدَ شَمْسٍ** — a muḍāf munādā, rightly manṣūb | Yes → could be badal |
| **نَوْفَلًا** | **يَا نَوْفَلًا** | **No** — it should be **يَا نَوْفَلُ** |

!!! important "Verdict"
    **نَوْفَل** is a single-word name. Put **يَا** in front of it and it drops its tanwīn but has **no reason to become manṣūb** — it should be **يَا نَوْفَلُ**. The test fails at Nawfal.

    Since **عَبْدَ شَمْسٍ** and **نَوْفَلًا** are joined by **وَ**, they must be the same kind of thing. Therefore **both** are **عَطْف البَيَان** for **أَخَوَيْنَا** — neither can be badal. The manṣūb **نَوْفَلًا** is itself the sign.

### 3.5 Discussion — Why Does نَوْفَلًا Follow Blindly?

!!! question "The Student's Objection"
    A student asked: **أَخَوَيْنَا** is only manṣūb because it is a compound (muḍāf) munādā — functionally, it isn't an object in the sentence. So why should **نَوْفَل** be manṣūb?

!!! important "The Answer — ʿAṭf al-Bayān Follows Blindly; Badal Asks Why"
    - **ʿAṭf al-bayān cannot exist in a vacuum.** It is always in a sentence, and in this sentence **أَخَوَيْنَا** is manṣūb — because of the **يَا**. The nidāʾ **is** an ʿāmil, just like a **فِعل** or a **حَرف** such as **إِنَّ / أَنَّ**. That is function enough.
    - **عَطْف البَيَان follows blindly.** Nawfal says: *"أَخَوَيْنَا is manṣūb for whatever reason — I will just become manṣūb myself."* It does not look at the *why*.
    - **بَدَل asks why.** Because the ʿāmil is repeated, a badal would be **يَا أَخَوَيْنَا ... يَا نَوْفَلُ** — as if another **يَا** stood between. Each badal gets its **own** effect from that ʿāmil. Nawfal would say: *"This naṣb is only because of the yā, and I don't become manṣūb under yā — so I keep my نَوْفَلُ."*

    So if the line had been **يَا أَخَوَيْنَا عَبْدَ شَمْسٍ وَنَوْفَلُ**, that would signal a **badal**. **نَوْفَلًا** signals ʿaṭf al-bayān.

!!! example "A Student's Variation — يَا أَخَوَيْنَا بِلَالًا وَنَوْفَلًا"
    Run the test: remove **أَخَوَيْنَا** → **يَا بِلَالًا** — incorrect. So it **cannot be badal**; it is **عَطْف البَيَان**.

## 4. عَطْف البَيَان in the Quran

### 4.1 Sūrat al-Māʾidah (95) — كَفَّارَةٌ طَعَامُ مَسَاكِينَ

> **يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا لَا تَقْتُلُوا الصَّيْدَ وَأَنتُمْ حُرُمٌ ۚ وَمَن قَتَلَهُ مِنكُم مُّتَعَمِّدًا ... أَوْ كَفَّارَةٌ طَعَامُ مَسَاكِينَ ...**
>
> *"O you who believe, do not kill game while you are in the state of iḥrām. And whoever of you kills it intentionally … or an expiation: feeding the poor …"*

The āyah concerns people in **iḥrām**, for whom hunting is forbidden. Whoever does hunt must give an expiation (**كَفَّارَة**). And what is that kaffārah? **طَعَامُ مَسَاكِينَ** — feeding the poor.

!!! example "Analysis"
    **طَعَامُ** is **مُضَاف**, **مَسَاكِينَ** is **مُضَاف إِلَيه**. **طَعَامُ مَسَاكِينَ** is **explaining what the kaffārah is** — so although technically it could be a badal, it is more suitable to label it **عَطْف البَيَان**.

### 4.2 Sūrat al-Nūr (35) — شَجَرَةٍ مُبَارَكَةٍ زَيْتُونَةٍ

> **اللَّهُ نُورُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ ۚ مَثَلُ نُورِهِ كَمِشْكَاةٍ ... يُوقَدُ مِن شَجَرَةٍ مُّبَارَكَةٍ زَيْتُونَةٍ ...**
>
> *"Allah is the Light of the heavens and the earth. The example of His light is like a niche … lit from [the oil of] a blessed tree, an olive …"*

The lamp burns from the oil of a blessed tree. What is that blessed tree? **زَيْتُونَة** — the olive tree, which is **more specific** than "a blessed tree." So it qualifies as **عَطْف البَيَان** — although people might call it badal, and since any ʿaṭf al-bayān can be a badal, there is no fight between the two here.

### 4.3 Sūrat Ibrāhīm (15–16) — مَاءٍ صَدِيدٍ

> **وَاسْتَفْتَحُوا وَخَابَ كُلُّ جَبَّارٍ عَنِيدٍ ۝ مِّن وَرَائِهِ جَهَنَّمُ وَيُسْقَىٰ مِن مَّاءٍ صَدِيدٍ**
>
> *"And they sought decision, and every obstinate tyrant came to nothing. Behind him is Hell, and he will be given to drink of a liquid — pus."*

| Word | Note |
|---|---|
| **اِسْتَفْتَحُوا** | They asked for victory / for a decision |
| **خَابَ** | He came to nothing — the **فِعل** |
| **كُلُّ** | **فَاعِل**; **جَبَّارٍ** is its **مُضَاف إِلَيه** |
| **جَبَّار** | An arrogant person, a tyrant, a dictator |
| **عَنِيد** | Obstinate — a **نَعت** for **جَبَّار** |
| **مَاء** | Here not "water" but **liquid** |
| **صَدِيد** | Pus |

!!! example "Why صَدِيدٍ Is عَطْف البَيَان"
    **صَدِيد** specifies and explains **what kind of liquid** every arrogant tyrant will be given to drink in Jahannam. It **can** be badal, but it can also be ʿaṭf al-bayān because it is explaining.

!!! question "Could صَدِيدٍ Be تَمْيِيز?"
    **No.** Tamyīz is singular and always **manṣūb**. Here **صَدِيدٍ** is **مَجرُور**, following **مَاءٍ** in its iʿrāb.

!!! note "Not a نَعت Either"
    Some may call it a naʿt — but meaning-wise **مَاءٍ صَدِيدٍ** as noun + adjective does not make sense. **صَدِيد** is a different liquid altogether, specifying what kind of liquid it is.

## 5. Back to the Book — Dr. Abdur Raheem's Study Method

## 6. Āyah 8 — Translation and al-Qurṭubī's Explanation

> **إِذْ قَالُوا لَيُوسُفُ وَأَخُوهُ أَحَبُّ إِلَىٰ أَبِينَا مِنَّا وَنَحْنُ عُصْبَةٌ إِنَّ أَبَانَا لَفِي ضَلَالٍ مُّبِينٍ**
>
> *"When they said: 'Indeed Yūsuf and his brother are more beloved to our father than we are, while we are a band. Indeed, our father is in clear error.'"*

### 6.1 إِذْ — Recap

As in Āyah 4, **إِذْ** means *when*, and there is an **implied verb** before it — something like **اُذْكُرْ** (*remember*). The whole sentence after **إِذْ** is the **مُضَاف إِلَيه** for it. *When they said* — who? The brothers of Yūsuf. See [Idh](../topics/idh-zarfiyya.md).

### 6.2 The Student's Translation

A student translated again:

- **لَ** in **لَيُوسُفُ** — the lām of emphasis: *indeed Yūsuf*
- **وَأَخُوهُ** — *and his brother*
- **أَحَبُّ** — **اِسم تَفضِيل**, comparison: *more beloved*
- **إِلَىٰ أَبِينَا مِنَّا** — *to our father than us*
- **وَنَحْنُ عُصْبَةٌ** — *and we are a group*
- **إِنَّ أَبَانَا لَفِي ضَلَالٍ مُّبِينٍ** — *indeed our father is in clear error*

### 6.3 From the Book — عُصْبَة and ضَلَال

!!! info "عُصْبَة"
    **عُصْبَةٌ مِنَ الرِّجَالِ مَا بَيْنَهُمْ ١٠ إِلَى ٤٠** — a group of men between 10 and 40: young, healthy, in the prime of youth — and a **group** on top of that. See [Usba](../topics/usba.md).

!!! info "ضَلَّ"
    To lose the way — to diverge or deviate from the path, so that one **never reaches** the destination. The **الضَّالِّين** of Sūrat al-Fātiḥah is from the same word. See [Root ḍ-l-l](../topics/root-dalla.md).

### 6.4 Not Error in Dīn

!!! important "What the Brothers Meant"
    We usually use **ضَال / ضَلَال** for people who have lost their way in matters of **dīn** or **īmān**. That is **not** the meaning here. Their father was not a normal person — he was a **Nabī**. Had they meant that, they would have lost their īmān — and we know that was not the case.

### 6.5 Al-Qurṭubī's Explanation (بِتَصَرُّف)

The book explains the meaning as:

> **الذَّهَابُ عَنْ وَجْهِ التَّدْبِيرِ فِي إِيثَارِ اثْنَيْنِ عَلَى عَشَرَةٍ مَعَ اسْتِوَائِهِمْ فِي الِانْتِسَابِ إِلَيْهِ**
>
> *Departing from sound judgement in preferring two over ten, despite their all being equal in their relation to him.*

| Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **الذَّهَابُ عَنْ وَجْهِ التَّدْبِيرِ** | Going away from the face of reason — deviating from **common sense** |
| **إِيثَار** | Favouring, preferring someone |
| **اثْنَيْنِ عَلَى عَشَرَةٍ** | The two (children) over the ten |
| **مَعَ** | Although / in spite of |
| **اسْتِوَائِهِمْ** | Their being **equal** |
| **الِانْتِسَاب إِلَيْهِ** | Their relationship to **him** — the father |

!!! info "إِيثَار"
    **إِيثَار** is favouring or preferring someone — which is why it is also the word for preferring someone **over your own self**: you need food or something else, and you give it away to someone else. That is called **إِيثَار**.

!!! info "اِسْتَوَى / يَسْتَوِي"
    To become **straight**, or to be **equal**. From the same root: **فَسَوَّاهَا** and **خَلَقَ فَسَوَّىٰ** — *He created, then made it proportioned/equal*. This word is used a lot about creation: Allah has made everything perfect, balanced. **اِسْتِوَاء** is being equal.

!!! info "اِنْتِسَاب"
    From **نَسَب** — relation, lineage.

!!! example "The Reasoning, Unpacked"
    They are all equal in their relationship to the father — they are **all his children**. It is not as though the two are his children and the ten are, say, his nephews; then preferring his own children would make sense. Yet he prefers these two over the ten. In doing so, the brothers think, he is departing from the correct understanding — going **against common sense**.

!!! note "Why This Passage Is Harder"
    **قَوْلُ القُرْطُبِي بِتَصَرُّف** — this portion is taken from **al-Qurṭubī's tafsīr** with some adaptation: the author has simplified it a bit. Al-Qurṭubī's tafsīr was written hundreds of years ago in a very concise style, so it takes some getting used to and some decoding.

!!! question "Homework"
    Go over this passage at home and **analyse it grammatically**. Unpack it: figure out the relationship between the words and how the sentences are constructed. It will help a great deal in future studies.

## 7. حَبَّ vs أَحَبَّ

### 7.1 The Verb

!!! note "From the Book"
    **حَبَّهُ** (Form I) — *he loved him*. But **أَحَبَّ** (Form IV) is the **more common** word for loving.

    > **وَهَذَا الفِعْلُ قَلِيلُ الاِسْتِعْمَالِ، وَكَثُرَ اسْتِعْمَالُ (أَحَبَّ) مِنْ بَابِ أَفْعَلَ**
    >
    > *This verb is seldom used; the usage of أَحَبَّ, from bāb afʿala (Form IV), is more frequent.*

!!! example "Parsing كَثُرَ اسْتِعْمَالُ (أَحَبَّ)"
    - **كَثُرَ** — the verb
    - **اسْتِعْمَالُ** — **فَاعِل**
    - **أَحَبَّ** — **مُضَاف إِلَيه** — but it is not declined, because it is being mentioned **as a word**; that is why the author put it in brackets.

!!! tip "Nouns Sometimes, Verbs Sometimes — Building Intuition"
    Notice how the author says **قَلِيلُ الاِسْتِعْمَالِ** (a noun phrase) in one clause, and **كَثُرَ اسْتِعْمَالُ** (a verb) in the next — rather than, say, **قَلَّ اسْتِعْمَالُ**. Arab writers use nouns sometimes and verbs sometimes. We do not write language like a mathematical formula.

    This is what builds **intuition** for the language: where to use a verb, where a noun, what a well-formed sentence is. You pick it up at a subconscious level if you **read** and **listen** to lots of quality Arabic content. Then you can *feel* whether something is well written — the same way we do in English or any other language, without dissecting it grammatically.

### 7.2 The Words for "Beloved"

The teacher compared words from this root meaning *beloved*, noting that although they share the passive sense, in usage they cover different kinds of love:

| Word | Typical usage |
|---|---|
| **مَحْبُوب** | Usually more the **romantic** kind of love |
| **حَبِيب** | **Affection** — you can call your parents, your children, all of them **حَبِيب** |
| **أَحِبَّاء** (pl.) | Used by the Jews for themselves: **نَحْنُ أَبْنَاءُ اللَّهِ وَأَحِبَّاؤُهُ** — *we are Allah's children and His beloved* |

### 7.3 The Maṣdar — A Crossover

!!! important "Form I Maṣdar with a Form IV Verb"
    - The **maṣdar of Form IV**, **إِحْبَاب**, is **almost never used**.
    - The **Form I verb**, **حَبَّ**, is **hardly used**.
    - So what happens: the **maṣdar of Form I (حُبّ)** is used with the **verb of Form IV (أَحَبَّ)**.

    Whenever you want the maṣdar — as a **مَفْعُول مُطْلَق** or simply as a regular noun — you use **حُبّ**, not **إِحْبَاب**.

!!! example "Sūrat al-Fajr (20)"
    > **وَتُحِبُّونَ الْمَالَ حُبًّا جَمًّا**
    >
    > *"And you love wealth with immense love."*

    **تُحِبُّونَ** is **Form IV** — you can see the **ḍammah** on the muḍāriʿ prefix. But the **مَفْعُول مُطْلَق** is **حُبًّا**, from **Form I** — not **إِحْبَابًا**. See [Mafool Mutlaq](../topics/mafool-mutlaq.md).

## 8. لَام الاِبتِدَاء

### 8.1 Its Name and Function

When the lām comes at the **beginning** of the sentence, it is called **لَام الاِبتِدَاء**. Its function is **تَوكِيد** — to **emphasise** the mubtadaʾ, the topic being discussed in the sentence.

!!! example "لَيُوسُفُ وَأَخُوهُ أَحَبُّ إِلَىٰ أَبِينَا"
    The brothers are **very emphatic**: *Indeed Yūsuf and his brother are more beloved to our father.*

The book gives further examples, which the teacher went over quickly, as the class has seen **لَام الاِبتِدَاء** many times.

### 8.2 The Ḥadīth — غَدْوَة and رَوْحَة

> **لَغَدْوَةٌ فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ أَوْ رَوْحَةٌ خَيْرٌ مِنَ الدُّنْيَا وَمَا فِيهَا**
>
> *"A forenoon or an afternoon [spent] in the path of Allah is better than the world and whatever is in it."*

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **غَدْوَة** | The **forenoon** — the time between morning and noon, before **زَوَال** |
| **رَوْحَة** | The **late afternoon** / evening — the time after noon |
| **غَدَاء** | A related word: in modern usage, **lunch**; in classical usage, **breakfast** |

!!! tip "The Weight of It"
    Just **one** forenoon or **one** afternoon in the path of Allah — not even a whole day — is better than this world and everything in it.

!!! question "Homework — The Root غ-د-و"
    - There is another related word: **look it up in the Quran** and find its meaning.
    - Then go to **مَقَايِيس اللُّغَة** — which gives the root meanings for every word and shows how the different variations are related — and figure out **what is common** between all of these words.

### 8.3 مُتَّفَقٌ عَلَيْهِ

!!! info "Ḥadīth Terminology"
    - **مُتَّفَقٌ عَلَيْهِ** — *agreed upon*, by both Imāms, **al-Bukhārī** and **Muslim**. Any ḥadīth in Bukhārī or Muslim is known to be ṣaḥīḥ; one that is **muttafaqun ʿalayh** is a level beyond — the highest authenticity certificate we have.
    - **رَوَاهُ البُخَارِيُّ فِي الجِهَادِ** — **رَوَى** means *to narrate*; **هُ** (the mafʿūl bih) refers to this ḥadīth; **البُخَارِيُّ** is the fāʿil. He narrated it in **كِتَاب الجِهَاد** — he divided his book into chapters.
    - **وَمُسْلِمٌ فِي الإِمَارَةِ** — and Muslim narrated it in the book of **الإِمَارَة** (leadership), followed by the ḥadīth numbers.

## 9. اللَّام المُزَحلَقَة — The Skidding Lām

### 9.1 The Book's Rule

> **وَإِذَا دَخَلَتْ إِنَّ عَلَى المُبْتَدَإِ انْتَقَلَتْ هَذِهِ اللَّامُ إِلَى خَبَرِهَا، وَتُسَمَّى حِينَئِذٍ اللَّامَ المُزَحْلَقَةَ لِأَنَّهَا زُحْلِقَتْ مِنْ مَكَانِهَا إِلَى خَبَرِ إِنَّ، كَمَا فِي: إِنَّ أَبَانَا لَفِي ضَلَالٍ مُبِينٍ**
>
> *When إِنَّ enters upon the mubtadaʾ, this lām transfers to its khabar, and it is then called the "skidded lām", because it was slid from its place to the khabar of إِنَّ — as in: indeed our father is in clear error.*

!!! example "Word Notes"
    - **اِنْتَقَلَ / يَنْتَقِلُ / اِنْتِقَال** — *to transfer, move*. A student said Form VII — it **looks** like Form VII (**اِنْفَعَلَ**), but the root is **ن-ق-ل**, so it is **Form VIII (اِفْتَعَلَ)**. If the root were **ت-ق-ل**, it could be Form VII. (In Urdu, **اِنتِقال** is used for someone passing away.) See [Iftaala Pattern](../topics/iftaala-pattern.md).
    - **اِنْتَقَلَتْ** — its **fāʿil** is **هَذِهِ**; **اللَّامُ** is a **بَدَل** — a demonstrative followed by a word with **ال** is badal, just like **هَذَا الكِتَابُ**.
    - **خَبَرِهَا** — the **هَا** refers to **إِنَّ** (or the mubtadaʾ) — the lām moves to the khabar of **إِنَّ**.
    - **تُسَمَّى** — **مَجْهُول** (passive): *it is called*.
    - **حِينَئِذٍ** — made up of **حِينَ** (a **ظَرف**) + **إِذٍ**: *at that time*.
    - **زَحْلَقَ** — *to skid, to slide*. The teacher mentioned that ice-skating blades, which you use to slide and skid, are called by a related word.

!!! note "A Rule We Know Well"
    Whenever **إِنَّ** enters, the lām slides and attaches itself either to the **khabar** — or, if the khabar comes first, to the **ism**.

!!! example "Sūrat al-Naḥl (18)"
    > **وَإِن تَعُدُّوا نِعْمَةَ اللَّهِ لَا تُحْصُوهَا ۗ إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَغَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ**
    >
    > *"And if you should count the favours of Allah, you could not enumerate them. Indeed, Allah is Forgiving and Merciful."*

    **لَغَفُورٌ** — lām al-muzaḥlaqah, because of **إِنَّ**. Remove **إِنَّ**, and the lām can come back to the start.

### 9.2 When the Khabar Is Fronted

!!! important "Rule"
    If the **khabar of إِنَّ** is a **شِبْهُ جُمْلَة** and it comes **before** the ism, the lām al-muzaḥlaqah attaches itself to the **delayed ism** (**اِسْمُهَا المُؤَخَّر**).

!!! example "Sūrat al-Nāziʿāt (26)"
    > **إِنَّ فِي ذَٰلِكَ لَعِبْرَةً لِّمَن يَخْشَىٰ**
    >
    > *"Indeed, in that is a lesson for whoever fears [Allah]."*

    - **فِي ذَٰلِكَ** — **جَارّ وَمَجرُور**, **شِبْه جُمْلَة**, the **khabar of إِنَّ** (fronted)
    - **عِبْرَةً** — the **ism of إِنَّ**, manṣūb — and the lām attaches to it

!!! tip "The Simple Rule"
    The lām goes and attaches itself to **whatever comes later** — ism or khabar.

### 9.3 When ضَمِير الفَصل Stands Between

> **وَإِذَا تَوَسَّطَ بَيْنَ اسْمِهَا وَخَبَرِهَا ضَمِيرُ الفَصْلِ ...**
>
> *And if the pronoun of separation comes in the middle, between its ism and its khabar …* — the lām enters upon the **ضَمِير الفَصل**.

!!! example "Parsing تَوَسَّطَ"
    - **تَوَسَّطَ** — **Form V** (**تَفَعَّلَ**), root **و-س-ط** (*middle*).
    - Who comes between the ism and the khabar? **ضَمِيرُ الفَصْلِ** — that is the **fāʿil**, delayed. The fāʿil and its fiʿl can be separated by many things in a sentence. (The teacher was rearranging the sentence to make it easier to follow.)

#### What Is ضَمِير الفَصل?

!!! info "Removing an Ambiguity"
    **ضَمِير الفَصل** is used where there could be an **ambiguity**: that the khabar might be taken for a **badal** (or a naʿt).

    Take **ذَٰلِكَ الكِتَابُ**:

    - **ذَٰلِكَ** could be the mubtadaʾ and **الكِتَابُ** the khabar — *That is the book.*
    - But it also looks like a badal — *That book …* — which is **not** a complete sentence.

    To say unambiguously *that is the book*, insert **هُوَ**: **ذَٰلِكَ هُوَ الكِتَابُ**. Now no one can mistake **الكِتَابُ** for a badal of **ذَٰلِكَ**.

!!! example "ذَٰلِكَ الْكِتَابُ لَا رَيْبَ فِيهِ"
    In the opening of Sūrat al-Baqarah, **ذَٰلِكَ الْكِتَابُ** has been translated both ways. Some grammarians are of the opinion: *That is the Book; there is no doubt in it.* Others: *That Book — there is no doubt in it.*

    **The teacher's caveat:** *"I'm not saying the Quran is ambiguous."* In the Quran — or in Arabic generally — something may be deliberately put this way when **both meanings could be meant**. If someone wanted to really pin the meaning down, they would use a **faṣl** — and there are many examples of faṣl in the Quran.

See [Dameer al-Fasl](../topics/dameer-al-fasl.md).

#### The Book's Example — Sūrat al-ʿAnkabūt (64)

> **وَمَا هَٰذِهِ الْحَيَاةُ الدُّنْيَا إِلَّا لَهْوٌ وَلَعِبٌ ۚ وَإِنَّ الدَّارَ الْآخِرَةَ لَهِيَ الْحَيَوَانُ ۚ لَوْ كَانُوا يَعْلَمُونَ**
>
> *"And this worldly life is nothing but diversion and play. And indeed, the Home of the Hereafter — that is the [true] life, if only they knew."*

!!! example "Analysis"
    - **هَٰذِهِ الْحَيَاةُ** — again a demonstrative followed by **ال**, so **الْحَيَاةُ** is **بَدَل**; **الدُّنْيَا** — its naʿt.
    - **إِنَّ الدَّارَ الْآخِرَةَ** — **الدَّارَ** is the ism of **إِنَّ**
    - **لَهِيَ** — **ضَمِير الفَصل**, with the **lām al-muzaḥlaqah** attached to it
    - **الْحَيَوَانُ** — the khabar

### 9.4 لَهْو vs لَعِب

!!! info "Two Kinds of Frivolity"
    Both mean useless, frivolous play. A distinction the teacher had read:

    - **لَعِب** — the baseless, frivolous things that **children** do, before puberty.
    - **لَهْو** — useless, frivolous things that have **desires** attached to them — what a youngster or teenager might do.

    And older people can, of course, exhibit both kinds of frivolity.

### 9.5 الحَيَوَان — Life at Its Most Intense

!!! info "The -ān Ending"
    Anything ending in **ـَان** in Arabic carries **extravagance, intensity**:

    | Word | Sense |
    |---|---|
    | **جَوْعَان** | *I am dying of hunger* |
    | **غَضْبَان** | *So, so angry — seeing red* |
    | **الرَّحْمَٰن** | Mercy like a **bottomless ocean** with huge waves forming in it |

    **الرَّحْمَٰن** captures the intensity of Allah's raḥmah. In the same way, **الحَيَوَان** captures the **intensity of life**: the Home of the Hereafter is the one that is **alive, active, throbbing, full of life**, and lasts forever.

!!! example "The Architect's Model"
    When a big building project is announced — a new residential block — the estate agent's office has a small **model** showing how the whole building will look once it is done. No matter how closely it resembles the real building, **it is not real**. It is nowhere near the real thing and has no value compared to it.

    That is this dunyā: a preliminary play-thing, nothing real compared to the intensity of life in **الدَّار الآخِرَة**.

## 10. Ibn Mālik on لَام الاِبتِدَاء

> **وَبَعْدَ ذَاتِ الكَسْرِ تَصْحَبُ الخَبَرْ** ⁂ **لَامُ ابْتِدَاءٍ نَحْوُ إِنِّي لَوَزَرْ**
>
> *"And after the one with the kasrah, the lām al-ibtidāʾ accompanies the khabar — as in: indeed I am a refuge."*

### 10.1 Why "Lām al-Ibtidāʾ"?

Ibn Mālik calls it **لَام الاِبتِدَاء**, because that is its **original** name (**الأَصل**). We only call it **المُزَحلَقَة** after **إِنَّ**; its real name is lām al-ibtidāʾ.

### 10.2 Two Rules in One Line

!!! important "ذَاتُ الكَسْر"
    **ذَاتُ الكَسْر** — *the one with the kasrah* — is **إِنَّ**, whose hamzah has a kasrah. In one very short line, Ibn Mālik gives two rules:

    1. After **إِنَّ**, the lām **accompanies the khabar**.
    2. This happens **only after إِنَّ** — **not after أَنَّ**, which has a fatḥah on its hamzah.

!!! example "Parsing the Line"
    Rearranged: **وَبَعْدَ ذَاتِ الكَسْرِ تَصْحَبُ لَامُ ابْتِدَاءٍ الخَبَرَ**

    - **تَصْحَبُ** — the fiʿl
    - **لَامُ ابْتِدَاءٍ** — the **fāʿil** — coming in the second line, because in poetry things are rearranged to fit the metre
    - **الخَبَرَ** — the mafʿūl bih

### 10.3 The Footnote

!!! note "From the Book's Footnote"
    **لَام الاِبتِدَاء** was originally lām al-ibtidāʾ, entering upon the mubtadaʾ. It enters upon **the khabar of إِنَّ مَكْسُورَة الهَمزَة** only. It does **not** enter upon the khabar of **أَنَّ مَفْتُوحَة الهَمزَة**.

    | Correct | Incorrect |
    |---|---|
    | **إِنِّي لَمُجْتَهِدٌ** | ~~**أَنِّي لَمُجْتَهِدٌ**~~ |

    It is necessary to warn about this: you **cannot** use the lām al-muzaḥlaqah with **أَنَّ**.

### 10.4 وَزَر — Refuge

!!! info "وَزَر = مَلْجَأ"
    **مَلْجَأ** is someone you take refuge with — the one you ask to protect you. Hence the beautiful duʿāʾ recited before going to bed:

    > **لَا مَلْجَأَ وَلَا مَنْجَا مِنْكَ إِلَّا إِلَيْكَ**
    >
    > *"There is no refuge and no escape from You except to You."*

    **مَنْجَا** — somewhere/someone that saves you — a similar meaning.

*"We survived another piece of poetry."*

## 11. Student Question — إِنِّي مُجْتَهِدٌ vs إِنِّي لَمُجْتَهِدٌ

!!! question "Is There Any Difference in Meaning?"
    Both **إِنِّي مُجْتَهِدٌ** and **إِنِّي لَمُجْتَهِدٌ** are acceptable. Is there a difference?

!!! summary "The Four Levels of Emphasis"
    | Level | Form | Sense |
    |---|---|---|
    | 1 | **أَنَا مُجْتَهِدٌ** | *I am hardworking* — no emphasis at all |
    | 2 | **لَأَنَا مُجْتَهِدٌ** | With the lām — one level of emphasis |
    | 3 | **إِنِّي مُجْتَهِدٌ** | *Indeed I am hardworking* |
    | 4 | **إِنِّي لَمُجْتَهِدٌ** | **إِنَّ + لَام** — the **highest** level of emphasis in Arabic |

!!! important "Reserved for Refutation"
    The highest level is usually reserved for the face of **refutation** — you are in a debate and the other person says *"no, you are not hardworking,"* so you come back strongly. You *can* use it where there is no refutation, but it won't be considered good Arabic.

!!! tip "Beyond Grammar"
    As far as **grammar** is concerned, both sentences are correct and equal. Deciding which is preferable — when to emphasise, how much — is **outside the realm of grammar**. That is where **rhetoric (البَلَاغَة)** starts: given two grammatically correct sentences, when to use one over the other depends on the context and the person you are talking to.

## 14. Key Lessons from This Session

!!! tip "Summary of Lessons"
    1. **عَطْف البَيَان** has a second element that is **better known or clearer**; in **بَدَل** the two elements are at a similar level of clarity.
    2. Rather than "in ʿaṭf al-bayān the first element is the concern," think: **in badal the second element is the concern — and in ʿaṭf al-bayān that may not be the case.**
    3. **Every ʿaṭf al-bayān can be a بَدَل كُلّ مِن كُلّ**, ~95% of the time — but not every badal can be ʿaṭf al-bayān.
    4. A **badal is two sentences under the hood**: its ʿāmil is implicitly repeated. **ʿAṭf al-bayān repeats no ʿāmil.**
    5. **The litmus test**: remove each element in turn. If both remaining sentences are correct, it can be badal; if either breaks — in meaning (**زَيْنَبُ جَاءَنِي بِلَالٌ**) or in grammar (**يَا العَبَّاس**) — it must be ʿaṭf al-bayān.
    6. **ʿAṭf al-bayān follows blindly; badal asks why.** That is why **نَوْفَلًا** is manṣūb after **أَخَوَيْنَا** — a badal would have been **نَوْفَلُ**.
    7. **Names that began as adjectives** (العَبَّاس، الحَسَن، الحُسَيْن) can take ال — but only those the Arabs actually used that way.
    8. In the Quran, **طَعَامُ مَسَاكِينَ**, **زَيْتُونَةٍ**, and **صَدِيدٍ** are best labelled ʿaṭf al-bayān because they **explain** what precedes them; **صَدِيدٍ** cannot be tamyīz, which is always manṣūb.
    9. The brothers' **ضَلَال** was a charge of **abandoning common sense** — preferring two over ten, though all were equally his children — not error in dīn.
    10. **Form I حَبَّ** is rare and **Form IV إِحْبَاب** is rare, so Arabic pairs **أَحَبَّ** with the maṣdar **حُبّ**: **وَتُحِبُّونَ الْمَالَ حُبًّا جَمًّا**.
    11. Good writers alternate **nouns and verbs**; intuition for this comes from reading and listening to lots of quality Arabic, not from formulas.
    12. **The lām al-muzaḥlaqah attaches to whatever comes later** — the khabar, a delayed ism, or a **ضَمِير الفَصل** (**لَهِيَ الْحَيَوَانُ**).
    13. **ضَمِير الفَصل** removes the ambiguity between a khabar and a badal: **ذَٰلِكَ هُوَ الكِتَابُ**.
    14. The **ـَان** ending conveys intensity: **الحَيَوَان** is life at full intensity, beside which this dunyā is only an architect's model.
    15. Per **Ibn Mālik**: the lām's original name is **لَام الاِبتِدَاء**, and it slides to the khabar **only after إِنَّ** — never after **أَنَّ**.
    16. There are **four levels of emphasis**; **إِنَّ + لَام** is the strongest and belongs to refutation. Choosing between grammatically correct options is the work of **البَلَاغَة**.
    17. **Attempt exercises before checking answers** — mistakes teach more than correct answers copied.
