# قَبْلُ and بَعْدُ — The Three-Case Rule

**قَبْل** (*before*) and **بَعْد** (*after*) are **ظَرْف زَمَان** — adverbs of time. Their ending tells you something precise about what has happened to their **مُضَاف إِلَيْه**, and the rule has three distinct cases.

---

## The Rule at a Glance

| مُضَاف إِلَيْه | Status of the word | Ending | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mentioned** | **مُعْرَب** — declines normally | كَسْرَة after مِن | **مِن قَبْلِهِ** |
| **Omitted but intended** | **مَبْنِي عَلَى الضَّمّ** | ضَمَّة | **مِن قَبْلُ** |
| **Neither mentioned nor intended** | **مُعْرَب**, and given tanwīn | ـًا | **قَبْلًا / بَعْدًا** |

---

## Case 1 — Omitted **but Intended** → مَبْنِي عَلَى الضَّمّ

!!! important "Two Conditions Together"
    **قَبْلُ** and **بَعْدُ** are built on the **ضَمَّة** when:

    1. the **مُضَاف إِلَيْه** is **omitted** (**حُذِفَ المُضَافُ إِلَيْه**); **and**
    2. it is nevertheless **intended** (**نُوِيَ مَعْنَاهُ**) — you *do* mean "before *that particular thing*," you simply do not say it.

!!! example "A Simple Illustration"
    > **أَنَا الآنَ مُدَرِّسٌ، وَكُنْتُ مِنْ قَبْلُ إِدَارِيًّا**
    >
    > *"I am now a teacher; before that I was an administrator."*

    "Before *that*" — before being a teacher — is fully intended, just not spelled out.

### Quranic Evidence

!!! example "Sūrat Yūsuf 12:6 — the case that prompted the discussion"
    > **كَمَا أَتَمَّهَا عَلَىٰ أَبَوَيْكَ مِن قَبْلُ**

    *"…before this,* He completed the same favour upon your fathers." The "before **this**" is clearly meant, and simply not stated.

!!! example "سُورَة الرُّوم — two instances in one āyah"
    > **لِلَّهِ الْأَمْرُ مِن قَبْلُ وَمِن بَعْدُ ۚ وَيَوْمَئِذٍ يَفْرَحُ الْمُؤْمِنُونَ**

    The context is the Romans: defeated, but within a few years they will overpower the Persians. Both the defeat **and** the eventual victory belong to Allah's command — before it and after it.

!!! example "سُورَة يُونُس — آلْآنَ وَقَدْ عَصَيْتَ قَبْلُ"
    > **آلْآنَ وَقَدْ عَصَيْتَ قَبْلُ وَكُنتَ مِنَ الْمُفْسِدِينَ**

    Spoken to Firʿawn as he drowns and declares belief in the Lord of Mūsā. **قَبْلُ** unmistakably means **before this moment** — before the moment in which you now proclaim īmān.

!!! example "سُورَة القَصَص — أَوَلَمْ يَكْفُرُوا بِمَا أُوتِيَ مُوسَىٰ مِن قَبْلُ"
    > **أَوَلَمْ يَكْفُرُوا بِمَا أُوتِيَ مُوسَىٰ مِن قَبْلُ ۖ قَالُوا سِحْرَانِ تَظَاهَرَا وَقَالُوا إِنَّا بِكُلٍّ كَافِرُونَ**

    They had said *"if only he were given the like of what Mūsā was given"* — meaning the Tawrāh, or the miracles of Mūsā عليه السلام. Allah answers: *did you not already reject what Mūsā was given, before this?* Here **مِن قَبْلُ** can mean before the coming of the Quran **or** before the coming of the Messenger ﷺ — **both meanings are available**.

    Their own words condemn them: **سِحْرَانِ تَظَاهَرَا**, *"two pieces of magic supporting each other"* — the Tawrāh attesting the Quran and the Quran attesting the Tawrāh — followed by **إِنَّا بِكُلٍّ كَافِرُونَ**.

---

## Case 2 — Mentioned → مُعْرَب

When the **مُضَاف إِلَيْه** is actually present, the word declines completely normally:

> **مِن قَبْلِهِ** — the **هُ** is the muḍāf ilayhi, so **قَبْلِ** is simply **مَجرُور** with a kasrah. Not **مَبْنِي** at all.

---

## Case 3 — Neither Mentioned nor Intended → مُعْرَب with تَنوِين

Here the muḍāf ilayhi is omitted **and you do not even intend one** — you are not thinking "before *what*" at all. Then the word is **مُعْرَب** and takes **تَنوِين**:

> **قَبْلًا** — *previously* · **بَعْدًا** — *later*

!!! note "لَمْ يُنْوَ"
    The Arabic phrasing of the condition is **لَمْ يُنْوَ** — *"it was not intended."* The morphology: **نِيَّة** → **نَوَى / يَنْوِي** → passive **يُنْوَى** → jussive **لَمْ يُنْوَ**, the final weak letter dropping because of the **جَزْم** caused by **لَمْ**.

!!! example "Everyday Illustrations"
    > **كُنْتُ مُدَرِّسًا قَبْلًا** — *"I was a teacher previously"* — with no particular "before what" in mind.
    >
    > **سَأَزُورُكَ بَعْدًا إِنْ شَاءَ اللَّه** — *"I will visit you later"* — not after ṣalāh, not after dinner; just **later**.

**Iʿrāb:** **بَعْدًا** is **مَنصُوب** because it is a **ظَرْف زَمَان**. Even though "later" names no precise moment, it is still a unit of time.

!!! tip "English Equivalents"
    The best English renderings of this third case are exactly **"previously"** and **"later"** — words that carry no implied complement.

### Poetic Evidence (شَوَاهِد)

!!! example "فَسَاغَ لِيَ الشَّرَابُ وَكُنْتُ قَبْلًا"
    > **فَسَاغَ لِيَ الشَّرَابُ وَكُنْتُ قَبْلًا** ⁂ **أَكَادُ أَغَصُّ بِالْمَاءِ الفُرَاتِ**
    >
    > *"So the drink went down easily for me — and previously I would almost choke on sweet water."*

    **سَاغَ** = *to become easy to swallow*; its **فَاعِل** is **الشَّرَاب**. The poet was in such distress before that even sweet water made him splutter — whereas hot water goes down easily on the throat. Now that his state has changed, even the wine goes down easily. **قَبْلًا** here is simply "previously": before *what* is neither specified nor intended.

!!! example "وَنَحْنُ قَتَلْنَا الأُسْدَ أُسْدَ خَفِيَّةٍ"
    > **وَنَحْنُ قَتَلْنَا الأُسْدَ أُسْدَ خَفِيَّةٍ** ⁂ **فَمَا شَرِبُوا بَعْدًا عَلَى لَذَّةٍ خَمْرَا**
    >
    > *"And we — we killed the lions, the lions of Khafiyyah; so never after did they drink wine for pleasure."*

    **الأُسْد** is a plural of **أَسَد**; **خَفِيَّة** was a place of dense, thorny thicket where lions dwelt. The poet boasts of killing enemy warriors — "the lions of Khafiyyah."

    Note the emphasis in **وَنَحْنُ قَتَلْنَا**: **قَتَلْنَا** alone would suffice, since **نَا** already indicates the subject; opening with the detached **نَحْنُ** is emphatic — *it is* **we** *who killed them*.

    And **بَعْدًا** does **not** mean "after killing them." It means simply **thereafter, ever again** — they were never able to enjoy wine again. The muḍāf ilayhi is not intended, and the **تَنوِين** is exactly what tells us so.

    (The phrase **الأُسْدَ أُسْدَ خَفِيَّةٍ** raised the question of **عَطْف البَيَان** — a concept the class had not yet covered, deferred to a future session with this couplet as the worked example.)
