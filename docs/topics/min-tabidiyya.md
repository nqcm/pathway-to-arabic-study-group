# مِن التَّبعِيضِيَّة — The Partitive مِن

**مِن التَّبعِيضِيَّة** is the **مِن** that means **بَعْض** — *some of*. It restricts the action to a **part** of the noun that follows, not the whole of it.

---

## The Test — Add It and Watch the Meaning Shrink

| Without مِن | With مِن |
|---|---|
| **قَرَأْتُ الكِتَابَ** — *I read the book* (all of it) | **قَرَأْتُ مِنَ الكِتَابِ** — *I read from the book* (part of it) |
| **كُلِ الطَّعَامَ** — *eat the food* (all of it) | **كُلْ مِنْهُ** — *eat from it* (some of it) |

!!! note "Not the Same as مِن الزَّائِدَة"
    The partitive مِن genuinely **changes the meaning**; it cannot be removed without loss. That distinguishes it from **مِن الزَّائِدَة**, which is syntactically removable and adds emphasis rather than quantity. See [[min-ziyada]].

---

## Examples from the Quran

!!! example "وَمِمَّا رَزَقْنَاهُمْ يُنفِقُونَ"
    > **وَمِمَّا رَزَقْنَاهُمْ يُنفِقُونَ**
    >
    > *"…and from what We have provided them, they spend."*

    Among the qualities of the believers: they believe in the unseen, establish the prayer, and spend **from** what Allah has given them — **some of it**, not all of it.

!!! tip "Look at the Choice of Words"
    The teacher paused on the sheer courtesy of this construction. Everything we possess was given to us by Allah in the first place — and even so He does not ask us to spend all of it.

    *"If I give you $100 and then tell you to give that $100 to so-and-so, I am completely within my rights, because I gave you the $100. But no — Allah gives, and then asks only for* **some** *of it."*

!!! example "وَيُعَلِّمُكَ مِن تَأْوِيلِ الْأَحَادِيثِ (Yūsuf 12:6)"
    > **وَيُعَلِّمُكَ مِن تَأْوِيلِ الْأَحَادِيثِ**

    *"…and He will teach you* **from** *the interpretation of narrations"* — a portion of that knowledge, not the whole of it. See [[tawil]].
