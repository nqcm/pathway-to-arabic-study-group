# لَام الجُحُود — The Lām of Emphatic Negation

**لَام الجُحُود** comes after a negated **كَانَ** to add emphasis. It looks as if it makes the following muḍāriʿ manṣūb, which is why some charts list it among the lāms of naṣb.

---

## When It Comes

It comes only **after مَا كَانَ or لَمْ يَكُنْ**, and it is for **emphasis**. It is restricted: *"It's not a lām you can use willy-nilly anywhere."*

!!! example "Our Army Cannot Be Defeated"
    **مَا كَانَ جَيْشُنَا لِيُهْزَمَ** (reconstructed from the teacher's translation)

!!! note "Correction"
    In Surah Yusuf Session 13, **كَادَ** and other **أَفعَال نَاقِصَة** were also named. That was a slip: this lām comes only after a negated **كَانَ**.

## What Really Gives the Naṣb

| View | Analysis |
|---|---|
| On the surface | The lām makes the muḍāriʿ manṣūb |
| Dissected | A **hidden أَنْ** after the lām makes it manṣūb. The lām is a regular **حَرف جَرّ**, and the hidden **أَنْ** + verb is a [مَصدَر مُؤَوَّل](masdar-muawwal.md) in the jarr position |

Because of this, some books place it under naṣb and others reject that placement. **لَام التَّعلِيل** has the same analysis. See [A Map of the Lāms](lam-types.md).
