## 1. Zooming Out — The Map of the Ḥurūf

Last time the class said it would look at the different meanings of the **ḥarf jarr lām**. Before going there, the teacher stepped back to look at **ḥurūf in general**, *"because sometimes it helps if we zoom out and take a look at the bigger picture before we zoom in on certain things"*, using a chart put together that morning.

### 1.1 One Word, Two Things

English distinguishes *letters* from *particles*. Arabic uses the one name, **الحُرُوف**, for both.

| Term | Meaning | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **حُرُوف المَبَانِي** | The letters of the alphabet | The **building blocks** of words, from **بَنَى / يَبْنِي** (*to build*). Not our concern here. |
| **حُرُوف المَعَانِي** | Particles | Those which **contain a meaning themselves** or **alter the meaning of something else** |

!!! example "A Ḥarf That Alters Meaning"
    *I am writing* becomes *I am not writing* just by placing **لَا** before the verb. **لَا** is a **حَرف**, and it completely alters the meaning of another word.

### 1.2 Working and Non-Working Particles

There are **more than 30 kinds** of ḥurūf al-maʿānī, classified at a higher level into two groups.

**الحُرُوف العَامِلَة** are the agents: they have some effect on the **endings of their مَعمُول**.

| Family | Effect |
|---|---|
| **حُرُوف الجَرّ** | The maʿmūl is **majrūr** |
| The particles of naṣb | Give **naṣb** to the muḍāriʿ |
| The particles of jazm (such as **لَمْ** and **لَمَّا**) | Give **jazm** to the muḍāriʿ |
| **الحُرُوف المُشَبَّهَة بِالفِعل** (**إِنَّ، أَنَّ** and the like) | Make a noun behave like the object of a verb |

The second group **has no maʿmūl**: they do not change the endings of anything. (The captions lost both the group's name and the teacher's examples. It is called **غَيْر عَامِلَة** or **مُهْمَلَة** in the notes below.)

### 1.3 One Ḥarf, Several Categories

The categories **overlap**. The same ḥarf can appear in different places on the chart, and the **lām** is the clearest case: it can be a **ḥarf jarr**, it can give **jazm**, and it can be listed as giving **naṣb** to the muḍāriʿ. (That last one is disputed: see Section 2.4.)

See [حُرُوف المَبَانِي وَالمَعَانِي](../topics/huruf-mabani-maani.md).

## 2. The Map of the Lāms

### 2.1 What "No Effect" Means

!!! important "Grammatical Effect, Not Effect on Meaning"
    When we call a lām non-working, we mean it causes **no change in the iʿrāb**. We do **not** mean it leaves the meaning alone. *"You should be really clear on that."*

    Without the lām of emphasis, a sentence has no emphasis at all. Add it and the meaning has changed, **but as far as the iʿrāb goes, no change**. So we classify it as non-working.

### 2.2 The Non-Working Lāms

**1. لَام الاِبتِدَاء** — there for **emphasis**. It changes nothing anywhere in the sentence.

**2. A second lām the teacher did not need to explain** — the name is lost, but following the لَام الاِبتِدَاء it is most likely **اللَّام المُزَحلَقَة**, which the class has just covered. It also has no effect on iʿrāb.

**3. لَام الفَارِقَة** — *"who can tell me what was fāriqah?"*

There is a lightened form of **إِنَّ**, which is **إِنْ**. The problem is that **إِنْ** can also be the **إِن of negation**:

> **إِنْ أُرِيدُ إِلَّا الْإِصْلَاحَ** (Hūd 88)
>
> *"I do not wish except correction."*

When the emphatic **إِنْ** appears in its lightened form, we use this lām to distinguish it, and that is why it is called **الفَارِقَة**: it makes the **فَرْق** between the two.

> **وَإِن كُنتَ مِن قَبْلِهِ لَمِنَ الْغَافِلِينَ** (Yūsuf 3)

!!! example "Reading the Āyah"
    If this were the **إِن of negation**, the meaning would be *you were not, before this, one of the heedless*. That is not the meaning here. The meaning is: **indeed, before this revelation, before Allah taught you, you were one of the heedless**. The lām tells us this **إِنْ** is for emphasis.

    Take away the lām, and the iʿrāb of the sentence does not change. So it is non-working.

!!! question "Does the إِن of Negation Only Come with إِلَّا?"
    A student asked whether negative **إِنْ** comes only when followed by **إِلَّا**.

    *That is a very common pattern, but it's not necessary.* You can find the **إِن** of negation without **إِلَّا**, which is exactly why the confusion can arise and why we have the **لَام الفَارِقَة** to tell the two apart.

See [لَام الفَارِقَة](../topics/lam-al-fariqah.md).

**4. اللَّام المُوَطِّئَة لِلقَسَم** — the lām that **paves the way** for the oath. It enters upon a conditional **إِنْ**, as in **لَئِنْ** (Section 5.2), and tells you that there is a hidden **قَسَم**. The whole topic of oaths was covered thoroughly in a separate **three-part workshop**.

!!! note "Correction"
    In class, **لَّقَدْ كَانَ** (āyah 7) was given as the example here. That was a slip: the lām of **لَقَدْ** is the lām **in the reply to an oath**, item 5 below (see [Session 9](session-9.md)).

**5. لَام الجَوَاب** — **جَوَاب القَسَم** can take a lām: *by Allah, I love you* (**وَاللَّهِ لَأُحِبُّكَ**). The jawāb is attached to the lām. The lām of **لَّقَدْ كَانَ فِي يُوسُفَ وَإِخْوَتِهِ آيَاتٌ لِّلسَّائِلِينَ** (āyah 7) is this lām too, in the reply to an omitted oath. And the jawāb of particles like **لَو** and **لَوْلَا** can also take a lām. (The teacher showed an āyah from the page, *"second last ayah on the right-hand side"*; the captions lost which one.)

**6. لَام البُعْد** — *"the easy one."* A student guessed the lām that starts a sentence: *No, that is lām al-ibtidāʾ.* This is the lām of **distance**. We can say **ذَاكَ** for something far, but not that far, like **a tree down the lane**. For the **stars**, we say **ذَٰلِكَ**.

!!! tip "Do Your Own Overview"
    *"It's a good practice to go over and do these high-level overviews every now and then because we do tend to forget."*

    The list isn't comprehensive. The teacher will share the board. Keep it and do exercises with it: **find examples of all these kinds of lām whenever you are reading the Quran**.

### 2.3 The Working Lāms

**لَام عَامِلَة** — from the name you can tell that it has some effect on what follows.

**1. الجَارَّة** — the ordinary **ḥarf jarr**. It has **over 30 meanings**, and this is where the discussion began. The chart lists only the commoner ones; the rest do not occur in the Quran or are more literary.

**2. الجَازِمَة** — the lām that gives the verb its jazm, **لَام الأَمْر**: **لِيَكْتُبْ** — *he should write*.

> **إِذَا تَدَايَنتُم بِدَيْنٍ إِلَىٰ أَجَلٍ مُّسَمًّى فَاكْتُبُوهُ ۚ وَلْيَكْتُب بَّيْنَكُمْ كَاتِبٌ بِالْعَدْلِ** (al-Baqarah 282)
>
> *"When you contract a debt for a specified term, write it down; and let a scribe write it down between you in justice."*

**3. النَّاصِبَة** — but this one is debatable.

### 2.4 The Debate Over the Naṣb Lāms

The chart puts two lāms here, **لَام الجُحُود** and **لَام التَّعلِيل**.

!!! important "Not Really the Lām"
    A sentence such as **مَا كَانَ جَيْشُنَا لِيُهْزَمَ** looks as if the lām is making the verb manṣūb. Dissect it, and there is a **hidden أَنْ** after the lām doing the naṣb.

    So the **lām** is a regular **حَرف جَرّ**, and it makes the **مَصدَر مُؤَوَّل** (the hidden **أَنْ** + the verb) **majrūr**. This is why many people reject the label: some books list it as **نَاصِبَة**, others will not, *"because it's not the lām technically which is giving the naṣb."*

A student pointed out that **لَام التَّعلِيل** has the same hidden element. The teacher agreed: it also makes the muḍāriʿ manṣūb, and it is also really the hidden **أَنْ** doing it.

!!! info "What Was لَام الجُحُود?"
    *"It hasn't been long since we have done it."* It comes **after مَا كَانَ or لَمْ يَكُنْ**, and it is for **emphasis**: *our army cannot be defeated* (the sentence above is reconstructed from that translation).

    *"It's not a lām you can use willy-nilly anywhere."*

!!! note "Correction"
    In class, **كَادَ** and other **أَفعَال نَاقِصَة** were also named. That was a slip: this lām comes only after a negated **كَانَ**.

See [لَام الجُحُود](../topics/lam-juhud.md) and [A Map of the Lāms](../topics/lam-types.md).

### 2.5 The Jarr List — One Lām, Many Jobs

The jārrah list is the longest. (The teacher had placed **lām al-juḥūd** in it by mistake and crossed it out, so the numbers on the board are a bit off.)

First on it is **لَام التَّقوِيَة**, done a couple of sessions back. A student recalled its condition: when the mafʿūl comes before the fiʿl. The two reasons again: the **ʿāmil is delayed** from its maʿmūl, or the ʿāmil is **a derived form**, an ism fāʿil or a maṣdar. And it is not compulsory.

!!! important "Why This Chart Was Made"
    Last time a student was confused about whether lām al-taqwiyah is still a **ḥarf jarr**. It is. Now the chart shows why: there is **lām al-jārrah**, and within it there are 30-plus meanings it can impart. Lām al-taqwiyah is one of them. Even the one jārr lām can have **multiple functions and usages**.

## 3. لَام المِلك and شِبه المِلك

The other meanings will follow over the next two sessions, some of them quickly because they have been done already. Today: one, with a related one added on.

(The teacher checked the pronunciation with the class: **مُلْك** is a kingdom, and **مِلْك** is something you own.)

### 3.1 لَام المِلك

> **لِلَّهِ مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ** (e.g. Luqmān 26; the captions lost which occurrence)

*"Forget about grammar. What do you think the meaning is telling you about this lām?"* A student: *belonging*. Very nice: **to Allah belongs everything in the heavens and the earth**.

This is the **most common usage** of this lām: *this book belongs to so-and-so*; *this pen is mine* (**هَذَا القَلَمُ لِي**).

It is still in the jārrah category: **your normal ḥarf jarr**, except that the meaning now shows that something belongs to something.

### 3.2 شِبه المِلك

Not on the chart, but added here: **شِبه المِلك**, like the **شِبه الجُملَة** the class has already done.

!!! example "The Stable"
    What is an **إِصْطَبْل**? A student knew it, since Urdu uses the same word for *stables*, and the English word is similar.

    > **الإِصْطَبْلُ لِلْبَقَرِ** — *the stable is for the cows.*

    This does **not** mean that the stable belongs to the cows. It is for their use, for them to go and sleep in. We do say *the horses' stable* or *the cows' stable*, but we do not mean they are its owner.

Two other names for it:

| Name | Root | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **لَام الاِستِحقَاق** | ح-ق-ق | Shows a **right** over something. A wife has a right over her husband's money because Allah has given it to her; here, the cows have the right to this place. |
| **لَام الاِختِصَاص** | خ-ص-ص | **Specifying** something for something else |

*"Don't get drowned by all the terminology."*

!!! example "At the Airport"
    A place at an airport may be marked as being **for disabled people**. That place does not belong to them. It has been specifically reserved for their use.

!!! summary "The Fundamental Difference"
    | Lām | Meaning |
    |---|---|
    | **لَام المِلك** | Something **truly belongs** to whoever the lām comes on |
    | **شِبه المِلك** | They have **a right to it**, or it is **specifically for their use** |

See [لَام الجَار](../topics/lam-al-jar-meanings.md).

## 4. Āyah 13 — The Grammar

Back to the book. The teacher admitted to not having opened it since the last class, after two very long weeks with bugs in production and late meetings: *"please bear with me if I make mistakes."*

> **قَالَ إِنِّي لَيَحْزُنُنِي أَن تَذْهَبُوا بِهِ وَأَخَافُ أَن يَأْكُلَهُ الذِّئْبُ وَأَنتُمْ عَنْهُ غَافِلُونَ**
>
> *"He said, 'Indeed, it saddens me that you should take him, and I fear that a wolf would eat him while you are heedless of him.'"*

Yaʿqūb عليه السلام is not allowing Yūsuf to go, because he fears they will be busy with their own things and something might harm Yūsuf meanwhile. The vocabulary was done last time.

### 4.1 ال in الذِّئْب

As a very beginner you learn that **ال** makes something definite. But the class has come a long way: there are different kinds of **ال**, and not all of them make something definite.

This one is **ال الجِنسِيَّة**, referring generically to the **whole genus**.

!!! example "Humanity and Apples"
    - *Man was created ungrateful.* Allah does not mean any particular man; it is **humanity in general**.
    - *Apples are tastier than grapes.* *"That's very subjective, but…"* We are not talking about any particular apple. We are talking about apples in general and their characteristic of taste.

The purpose here is to explain the reality of the thing, its **مَاهِيَّة**. A student offered *essential nature*: *"very good."* It captures the attributes of the whole genus, not any individual.

!!! note "One Function Among Several"
    **Bayān al-māhiyyah** is **one function** of al-jinsiyyah. Not every al-jinsiyyah is for this.

(The teacher recalled a very good discussion, in a Telegram group for another class, about how to translate such expressions, and will try to find and share it.)

See [Types of ال](../topics/types-of-al.md).

### 4.2 The Lām of لَيَحْزُنُنِي

Because it comes with **إِنَّ**, this lām goes and attaches itself to what comes later, here the **khabar**. Sometimes, if the khabar comes first, it attaches itself to the **ism** of إِنَّ.

As you can see, **يَحْزُنُ** is still in its normal default state. The lām is making no change to it, which is exactly what the chart said: **it has no effect**.

See [لَامُ المُزَحلَقَة](../topics/lam-muzaalaqa.md).

### 4.3 أَن تَذْهَبُوا بِهِ — A Box in Place of a Maṣdar

**أَن تَذْهَبُوا بِهِ** can be treated like a normal maṣdar. Think of it as **a box sitting in place of a maṣdar**, which can be marfūʿ, manṣūb or majrūr depending on what it is doing.

Here it is in the place of the **فَاعِل**. If it were one word it would be **marfūʿ**, because it is a fāʿil. Open the box and inside you find **أَنْ** + a **manṣūb muḍāriʿ**, and possibly other things.

!!! info "مَصدَر مُؤَوَّل"
    Together they are called **المَصدَر المُؤَوَّل**: its meaning is **understood as** a maṣdar, but it is not a true maṣdar.

Substitute a real maṣdar and you get **يَحْزُنُنِي ذَهَابُكُمْ بِهِ**. The fāʿil is plain to see.

### 4.4 The Book's Examples — Converting to a True Maṣdar

The book gives further examples on the same pattern. The teacher took a small detour to notice what happens in each conversion.

(The wording is reconstructed from the teacher's translations.)

!!! example "It saddens me to see you sick"
    **أَنْ أَرَاكَ مَرِيضًا** → **رُؤْيَتِي إِيَّاكَ مَرِيضًا**

    Unpack **أَرَاكَ**: a **manṣūb muḍāriʿ**; its fāʿil is a hidden **أَنَا**; **كَ** is mafʿūl number one, and **مَرِيضًا** is number two, because the verb can take two (or it could be a **حَال**, depending on how you interpret it).

    Now look at the maṣdar. **ـِي** in **رُؤْيَتِي** is now a **مُضَاف إِلَيْهِ**. Before, the *I* was the **fāʿil**.

!!! important "What Happens to the Fāʿil"
    When you change a maṣdar muʾawwal into its normal maṣdar, what was the **fāʿil** usually turns into the **مُضَاف إِلَيْهِ**.

    *I see you* → *my seeing you*. *"Think of it in English or any language you are comfortable with, and it will just make sense."*

    And because **كَ** is a pronoun and cannot come on its own, we bring in **إِيَّا**. If it were any other noun, it would simply follow normally. **مَرِيضًا** comes along normally.

!!! example "It grieves my father that I should be lazy"
    **أَنْ أَكْسَلَ** → **كَسَلِي**

    The *I* was the fāʿil, and is now the muḍāf ilayh. The verb is **intransitive** and has no mafʿūl, so there is nothing to carry over.

!!! example "It saddens my mother that I should travel to this far country"
    **أَنْ أُسَافِرَ** → **سَفَرِي**

    Once again, the **ـِي** was the fāʿil in the original construction. (The book's word for *far* is lost in the captions.)

!!! tip "A Skill Worth Having"
    Being able to change a verb into a maṣdar, and a maṣdar into a verb, *"actually comes in really handy"* when you are stuck. It helps you understand any āyah or piece of poetry on the fly.

See [المصدر المؤوَّل](../topics/masdar-muawwal.md), [The Maṣdar's Muḍāf](../topics/masdar-mudaf.md) and [إيّا](../topics/iyyaa-pronouns.md).

### 4.5 وَأَخَافُ أَن يَأْكُلَهُ الذِّئْبُ — The Box as Mafʿūl Bih

Now work out what this maṣdar muʾawwal is doing. In the first one, what is doing the act of making him sad? *That you go.* So that one was the **fāʿil**.

In this one, we **already have** a fāʿil: *I fear*. But what is the thing being feared? This time the box is the **مَفعُول بِهِ**.

As before: **الذِّئْب** is the fāʿil of **يَأْكُلَ**, and when you bring it to a normal maṣdar the fāʿil becomes the **muḍāf ilayh**: **أَكْلَ الذِّئْبِ إِيَّاهُ**. The **هُ** is a pronoun and cannot come on its own, so **إِيَّا** comes in.

| With أَنْ + muḍāriʿ | With a true maṣdar |
|---|---|
| *I fear that I will fail* | *I fear failure*, or, keeping the fāʿil as muḍāf ilayh, *I fear my failure* |
| **نَخَافُ أَنْ نَمْرَضَ** — *we fear that we will fall sick* | **نَخَافُ المَرَضَ** or **نَخَافُ مَرَضَنَا** |
| *Do you fear that your workers will cheat you?* | *Do you fear your employees' cheating of you?* (the workers, the fāʿil, become the muḍāf ilayh) |

(The Arabic of the first and third sentences is lost in the captions.)

### 4.6 A Student's Question — Can't the Fāʿil Be Hidden?

!!! question "Could يَحْزُنُ Have a Hidden هُوَ as Its Fāʿil?"
    The teacher ran through it: **يَحْزُنُ** is the fiʿl, **ـِي** is the mafʿūl, the **نُون** is for protection. So where is the fāʿil?

    Remember **حَزِنَ** is *to become sad*, but **حَزَنَ** means *to make someone sad*: it is transitive. So *something* is making him sad. If we say the maṣdar muʾawwal is not the fāʿil, let's get rid of it for a minute, and the meaning is **incomplete**. He would be saying something is making him sad but not telling us what.

    If it were the other verb, *I am sad*, it would be different. But what is the thing making him sad? **That you go with him.**

See [حَزِنَ / حَزَنَ](../topics/verb-hazana.md).

## 5. Āyah 14 — لَئِنْ أَكَلَهُ الذِّئْبُ

> **قَالُوا لَئِنْ أَكَلَهُ الذِّئْبُ وَنَحْنُ عُصْبَةٌ إِنَّا إِذًا لَّخَاسِرُونَ**
>
> *"They said, 'If the wolf should eat him while we are a strong band, indeed, we would then be losers.'"*

A student translated well; *"quite straightforward."*

### 5.1 خَسِرَ

The book brings in **تَاجِر** (a merchant or trader) to make you understand that **خَسِرَ** means *a loss, as when one makes a loss in trade*.

| Usage | Meaning |
|---|---|
| In the context of trade | To **sustain a loss** |
| Outside trade, said of a person | To **be destroyed**: not a material loss, but a loss to yourself |

There are **three maṣdars**. Most likely (the captions lost the words):

- **خَسَارَة** — the common one that we use most of the time
- **خُسْر** and **خُسْرَان** — both appear in the Quran

!!! info "Choosing Between Maṣdars"
    The teacher has a book by **Dr. Fāḍil al-Sāmarrāʾī** whose specialty is what difference it makes when different maṣdars are used, with a passage running over a couple of pages on these. The teacher will find it, share it on the group, and see if the class can read it.

See [خَسِرَ](../topics/verb-khasira.md).

### 5.2 The Oath Marker in لَئِنْ

*"What is this? We have spent so much time on it, and we just saw it in our chart."* It is **اللَّام المُوَطِّئَة**, and it has no effect on the iʿrāb. What does it do to the meaning?

It is written as one word, but technically it is **lām + إِنْ**, and this **إِنْ** is **شَرْطِيَّة**. They are **taking an oath**. Had the oath been explicit, we would have **وَاللَّهِ** or any other oath. This lām tells us there is an implied **قَسَم** even though the oath has not been mentioned. As the book puts it, it enters upon the **إِن of condition** and stands in the place of the oath.

### 5.3 Sometimes the Lām Goes Too

The oath is omitted and the lām points to it. But sometimes **the lām is omitted along with the oath**:

> **قَالَا رَبَّنَا ظَلَمْنَا أَنفُسَنَا وَإِن لَّمْ تَغْفِرْ لَنَا وَتَرْحَمْنَا لَنَكُونَنَّ مِنَ الْخَاسِرِينَ** (al-Aʿrāf 23)
>
> *"They said, 'Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves, and if You do not forgive us and have mercy on us, we will surely be among the losers.'"*

!!! question "How Do We Know There Is an Oath Here?"
    There is no oath and no lām on the **إِن**. So what is the clue? **Why the emphasis?**

    We do not do this kind of emphasis with a normal sharṭ. If this were a normal sharṭ, it would just be **نَكُنْ**, majzūm because of **إِنْ**, with no point in having the lām of emphasis and all of that. This construction is **reserved for the jawāb of an oath**. So the jawāb **لَنَكُونَنَّ**, with the lām and the nūn, is telling us there was an oath, and a lām, dropped here.

### 5.4 Sometimes a Fāʿil Occurs Way Down the Lane

The book continues with **يَدُلُّ**: what is pointing to the oath being there? **What is the doer of this pointing?** It is the phrase with the lām and nūn, which comes well after the verb.

!!! tip "Ask Who Is Doing It"
    Sometimes a fāʿil can occur way down the lane, and you have to look for it. Ask yourself who is doing the act. If nothing nearby makes sense, ask whether the context gives you enough to understand a hidden fāʿil. Here it does not: the whole point of the sentence is to tell you *how we know there is an oath*, so we could not have known it if that word weren't mentioned.

The book then says to look at the explanations of **الآيَة التَّالِيَة**, *the next āyah* (**التَّالِيَة** is a **نَعت** for **الآيَة**), for more on the topic.

See [لَئِن](../topics/lain-combination.md).

## 6. When قَسَم and شَرْط Meet

The class covered this in the oaths workshop, so the teacher did not spend long on it, and the book's Arabic was simply read through. For more examples, go and watch that video.

### 6.1 Two Sets of Rules

Both the **qasam** and the **sharṭ** require a **jawāb**, and the two jawābs are constructed differently.

- **جَوَاب القَسَم**: a whole set of rules. A negative one needs no emphasis; an affirmative māḍī or muḍāriʿ is emphasised with particular things.
- **جَوَاب الشَّرْط**: its own rules. The muḍāriʿ has to become **majzūm**; a **جُملَة اِسمِيَّة**, or one with **سَ** or **سَوْفَ**, takes **فَ**; and so on.

So when they come together, whose rules apply?

!!! important "Whoever Comes First"
    - If the **qasam** is first, the jawāb follows the rules of the **qasam**.
    - If the **sharṭ** is first, the jawāb follows the rules of the **sharṭ**.
    - Whichever came **later**, its jawāb is **omitted**.

### 6.2 The Book's Series of Examples

(The predicate of these sentences is lost in the captions; the structure is what matters.)

| Case | Skeleton | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| Sharṭ alone | **إِنْ لَمْ تَفْعَلْ فَأَنْتَ …** | A plain sharṭ and jawāb. The jawāb is a jumlah ismiyyah, so it needs **فَ** |
| Qasam alone | **وَاللَّهِ إِنَّكَ لَـ…** | A jumlah ismiyyah as jawāb of a qasam takes **إِنَّ + lām** |
| Sharṭ first, then qasam | **إِنْ لَمْ تَفْعَلْ وَاللَّهِ فَأَنْتَ …** | Exactly like the first: **فَ** present, no emphasis |
| Qasam first, then sharṭ | **وَاللَّهِ إِنْ لَمْ تَفْعَلْ إِنَّكَ لَـ…** | **فَ** is gone; **إِنَّ + lām** appear |
| Shorthand for it | **لَئِنْ لَمْ تَفْعَلْ إِنَّكَ لَـ…** | Replace **وَاللَّهِ** with the lām. *Exactly the same thing.* |
| Lām removed too | **إِنْ لَمْ تَفْعَلْ إِنَّكَ لَـ…** | The jawāb still shows there is an oath |

!!! question "How Do We Know Whose Jawāb It Is?"
    The **فَ** tells us it is the jawāb of the **sharṭ**, because the jumlah is ismiyyah. The **absence of إِنَّ + lām** confirms it. Reverse both, and you have the jawāb of the oath.

That last row is exactly what is happening in al-Aʿrāf 23.

### 6.3 Why لَنَكُونَنَّ Has Both Tools

The book's rule: a jawāb that is a **jumlah fiʿliyyah**, starting with a **muḍāriʿ**, and **affirmative**:

!!! important "Compulsory"
    It is **compulsory** to emphasise it with **lām + nūn al-tawkīd**. *"Notice it's compulsory, it's not optional."*

**لَنَكُونَنَّ** is a jawāb, it is muḍāriʿ, and there is no negation, so both tools are needed.

!!! note "جَعَلَ in the Book's Sentence"
    *"When we turn this sentence into a jawāb…"*: the book uses **جَعَلَ**. Of its four meanings, this is **making something into something else**, as in **جَعَلْتُ بَيْتِي مَدْرَسَةً**, *I made my house into a school.*

!!! note "The Footnote — قَدْ يُكْتَفَى"
    The book's footnote uses **قَدْ** before a muḍāriʿ (it is passive, **يُكْتَفَى**, but still a muḍāriʿ). What does that imply? **Sometimes.** It might be enough with just **one** of the two emphases.

### 6.4 Back to Āyah 14

*"Let's try to think through things ourselves."*

**لَئِنْ** gives us the lām (for the oath) and **إِنْ** (for the condition). Leave **إِذًا** out for now. **إِنَّا … لَخَاسِرُونَ** has **إِنَّ** and **lām**: two emphases. So is the jawāb for the qasam or the sharṭ? **The qasam.** Had it been the jawāb of the sharṭ, it would have had a **فَ** and no emphasis at all.

The book says the āyah **contains** (**تَضَمَّنَ**) a qasam and a sharṭ. The qasam **comes first**, so its jawāb remains and the jawāb of the sharṭ is omitted. The same happens in al-Aʿrāf 23.

!!! summary "The Book's Rule"
    When the sharṭ and the qasam come together, omit the jawāb of **whichever is delayed**, and do so **لُزُومًا** — *compulsorily* (from the same root as **مُلْزَم**, something made compulsory).

See [Jawab al-Qasam](../topics/jawab-al-qasam.md) and [Jawāb al-Sharṭ](../topics/jawab-al-shart.md).

### 6.5 إِذًا

The book calls it a **حَرف جَوَاب وَجَزَاء**: it shows something happening because of something else, the *recompense* of that.

It gives **naṣb** to a **fiʿl muḍāriʿ**, under some conditions (done before).

!!! important "Remember the Chart"
    **إِذًا** is **عَامِلَة** only when it enters upon a muḍāriʿ, and then only with its conditions. When it enters upon anything else, it is **مُهْمَلَة** and has no effect.

    In this āyah it enters upon a **noun**, **لَّخَاسِرُونَ**, so it has no effect at all.

See [إِذَن](../topics/idhan-particle.md).

## 8. Āyah 15 — فَلَمَّا ذَهَبُوا بِهِ

> **فَلَمَّا ذَهَبُوا بِهِ وَأَجْمَعُوا أَن يَجْعَلُوهُ فِي غَيَابَتِ الْجُبِّ ۚ وَأَوْحَيْنَا إِلَيْهِ لَتُنَبِّئَنَّهُم بِأَمْرِهِمْ هَٰذَا وَهُمْ لَا يَشْعُرُونَ**
>
> *"So when they took him and agreed to put him into the bottom of the well, We inspired to him, 'You will surely inform them of this matter of theirs while they do not perceive.'"*

A student translated, with two sticking points:

- Who is the *him* to whom Allah is revealing? **Yūsuf** عليه السلام.
- **لَتُنَبِّئَنَّهُم**: *it's just the emphasis.* Remove the lām and the nūn of emphasis and you have **تُنَبِّئُهُمْ**, *you will inform them*.

!!! info "The Prophecy in the Well"
    When he was put in the well, Allah told him at that very moment that a time would come when he would confront them over this deed of theirs, and that **they would not even know it was him**. The same thing happened when Yūsuf عليه السلام rose to power and the brothers came to him, in the story yet to follow.

### 8.1 أَجْمَعَ

The verb can be used two ways: with a **direct mafʿūl**, or with **عَلَى**. In this āyah it is **direct**. Where is the mafʿūl? (*"Very nice."*) It is **أَن يَجْعَلُوهُ**, once again a maṣdar muʾawwal.

It means **to be firm on a decision**, to be decided.

!!! info "From Debate to Decision"
    In the previous āyāt there was discussion. One said *kill him* or *fling him into some land*. Another said *no, don't kill him; throw him into the recesses of the well*. Now Allah tells us they finally made this decision.

!!! example "The Ḥadīth on Fasting"
    > **مَنْ لَمْ يُجْمِعِ الصِّيَامَ قَبْلَ الْفَجْرِ فَلَا صِيَامَ لَهُ**
    >
    > *"Whoever has not resolved upon the fast before Fajr, there is no fast for him."*

    This is for the **farḍ** fasts: you must be decided in your heart that you are fasting tomorrow.

    - Say someone could not wake up for suḥūr but **already had the intention** of fasting. Even if they woke up after Fajr, the fast is valid.
    - If they were **undecided** until after Fajr had begun, making the intention then doesn't work.
    - For **nafl** fasts, a person can make the intention even after the day has started, as long as they have not eaten or drunk anything, and complete it until sunset.

**لَا** in **فَلَا صِيَامَ لَهُ**: the teacher asked what kind it is and whether its ism is manṣūb, and the answer was no (most likely **لَا النَّافِيَة لِلجِنس**, whose ism here is **مَبنِي**, not manṣūb; the captions are fragmentary here).

!!! note "The Plural Adds a Meaning"
    Used of a group, **أَجْمَعُوا** has the meaning of deciding with the added meaning of **all agreeing upon it**. Earlier, one brother was giving one suggestion and another a different one; by this āyah they have **all** agreed.

See [أَجْمَعَ](../topics/verb-ajmaa.md).

### 8.2 نَبَأ and نَبَّأَ

**نَبَأ** is news, but *very important* news, as the class has done before. It is different from **خَبَر**, the day-to-day things happening; **نَبَأ** is something huge.

> **عَنِ النَّبَإِ الْعَظِيمِ** (al-Nabaʾ 2; most likely the reference here)

Allah even gives **نَبَأ** an adjective, which heightens its magnitude further.

!!! info "نَبِيّ and نَبِيء"
    **نَبِيّ** is from this same root: someone informing you of something great. The root letters are **ن-ب-أ**, which is why in some qirāʾāt the word is **نَبِيء** rather than **نَبِيّ**: in some the hamzah has been assimilated into a yāʾ, and in others it is kept intact.

    The hamzah is kept in the qirāʾah of **Nāfiʿ**, in both of its transmissions, **Warsh** and **Qālūn**. **Ḥafṣ** reads **نَبِيّ**.

**نَبَّأَ** (Form II) means to inform somebody of something. The teacher wanted to check the base form, since sometimes a word's base form is hardly ever used.

See [نَبَأ vs خَبَر](../topics/naba-khabar.md).

### 8.3 أَمْر and شَأْن

A student had translated **بِأَمْرِهِمْ** as *their command*. Here **أَمْر** does not mean a command. It means **a matter**, which the book glosses with **شَأْن**.

> **كُلَّ يَوْمٍ هُوَ فِي شَأْنٍ** (al-Raḥmān 29)
>
> Every day He has a different matter, a different condition.

**شَأْن** is one of those words that are quite difficult to translate into one English word; you need a few descriptive words. Here, the easiest translation is just *the matter*.

!!! important "Two Meanings, Two Plurals"
    | أَمْر as | Plural |
    |---|---|
    | A **matter, condition** | **أُمُور** |
    | Something **ordained, a command** | **أَوَامِر** |

See [أَمْر](../topics/amr-matter-command.md).

### 8.4 جَعَلَ — To Put

Earlier in the session **جَعَلَ** meant making something into something else. Of the meanings of **جَعَلَ** the class has discussed, here it is **to put**: to put him in the well.

See [جَعَلَ](../topics/verb-jaala.md).

## 10. Key Lessons from This Session

!!! tip "Summary of Lessons"
    1. **حُرُوف المَعَانِي** either carry a meaning or alter another word's meaning; some are **عَامِلَة** and change endings, others are not.
    2. "No effect" means **no effect on iʿrāb**. Non-working particles can still change the meaning.
    3. The same ḥarf can sit in several categories. The **lām** is non-working, jārrah, jāzimah, and (disputedly) nāṣibah.
    4. **لَام الفَارِقَة** tells the emphatic **إِنْ** from the negating **إِنْ**.
    5. With **لَام الجُحُود** and **لَام التَّعلِيل**, a hidden **أَنْ** does the naṣb and the lām is really a ḥarf jarr on the maṣdar muʾawwal.
    6. **Lām al-taqwiyah is still a ḥarf jarr.** The jārr lām has 30-plus functions.
    7. **لَام المِلك** shows true ownership; **شِبه المِلك** shows a right or a designated use, like a stable for cows or a reserved airport seat.
    8. **ال الجِنسِيَّة** speaks of a genus, and may serve to show its **مَاهِيَّة**.
    9. **أَنْ + muḍāriʿ** is a box in place of a maṣdar: **fāʿil** in **أَن تَذْهَبُوا**, **mafʿūl bih** in **أَن يَأْكُلَهُ**.
    10. Converting to a true maṣdar, the **fāʿil becomes the muḍāf ilayh**, and an object pronoun comes through **إِيَّا**. Being able to convert helps you read on the fly.
    11. A transitive verb like **حَزَنَ** leaves the question *what is doing this?* That question finds the fāʿil, even one *way down the lane*.
    12. **خَسِرَ** is a loss in trade, and outside trade, destruction.
    13. **لَئِنْ** signals an oath. The lām can also be dropped, and the **form of the jawāb** still gives the oath away (al-Aʿrāf 23).
    14. When **qasam and sharṭ** meet, the jawāb belongs to **whichever comes first**; the later one's jawāb is compulsorily omitted.
    15. An affirmative muḍāriʿ jawāb of an oath **must** take **lām + nūn**.
    16. **إِذًا** gives naṣb only to a muḍāriʿ under its conditions; before a noun it has no effect.
    17. **Do the exercises, mark each other's work, argue, and only then check the key.**
    18. **أَجْمَعَ** is firm resolve; of a group, agreement. The obligatory fast requires it before Fajr.
    19. **نَبِيّ** comes from **نَبَأ**, momentous news; the qirāʾah of Nāfiʿ keeps the hamzah: **نَبِيء**.
    20. **أَمْر** as *matter* pluralises to **أُمُور**; as *command*, to **أَوَامِر**.
