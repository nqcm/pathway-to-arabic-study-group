# Surah Yusuf — Study Session 12

## Overview

The main topics covered in this session are:

- **لَام التَّقوِيَة** — what an **عَامِل** is, the **two reasons** an ʿāmil becomes weak (**تَأَخُّر العَامِل** and being a **فَرْع** in its work), and why the lām is always **optional**
- What **زَائِد** really means — extra **grammatically, never semantically** (**هَلْ مِن مَّزِيدٍ**)
- **The two-condition test**: telling lām al-taqwiyah apart from an ordinary prepositional lām (**أَرْسِلْهُ** glossed with **سَمَحَ لَهُ**)
- **Āyah 12** — **أَرْسِلْهُ مَعَنَا غَدًا يَرْتَعْ وَيَلْعَبْ وَإِنَّا لَهُ لَحَافِظُونَ**: **رَتَعَ** and **مَاشِيَة**, **حَفِظَ** and guarding the Quran, **غَد** and its dropped lām, **مَعَ vs عِنْدَ**, the hamzah of Form IV and its **أَمْر**, jazm in reply to a command
- **The book's own sentence** — **ضَعُفَ عَنِ العَمَلِ** as a nested **نَعت**; training the mind to ask for the **إِعرَاب of every jumlah**
- Quranic and book examples: **لِرَبِّهِمْ يَرْهَبُونَ** (with the story of Mūsā's **tablets**), **لِلرُّؤْيَا تَعْبُرُونَ**, **لَهُ لَحَافِظُونَ** (two reasons together), and lām al-taqwiyah vs **اللَّام المُزَحلَقَة**
- **Maṣdar and ism fāʿil without the lām** — **ذِكْرُ رَحْمَتِ رَبِّكَ عَبْدَهُ**, **مُخْلِفَ وَعْدِهِ رُسُلَهُ**; respectful terminology for Allah's name
- **Āyah 13** begins — **حَزِنَ / يَحْزَنُ vs حَزَنَ / يَحْزُنُ**, **ذَهَبَ** made transitive with **بِـ** or a **hamzah**, **شَكُور** said of Allah, the definition of **خَوْف**, **مُخِيف** from **مُخْوِف**, **ذِئْب**

## 1. A Detour to the Whiteboard — لَام التَّقوِيَة

The class had completed Āyah 11 and was due to begin Āyah 12. Before that, a detour to the whiteboard for a concept the day's notes depend on. A student remembered the teacher mentioning that a word can sometimes take a mafʿūl bih with a lām; another did not remember the topic at all — so the teacher went through it again.

### 1.1 The Name

**تَقْوِيَة** comes from **قَوِيَ** — *to be strong*. Bring it to **Form II**: **قَوَّى** — and the maṣdar is **تَقْوِيَة**.

!!! note "The Form II Maṣdar"
    The usual Form II maṣdar is **تَفْعِيل**. But when the verb is **نَاقِص** — its **last letter is weak** — we do not use that pattern; we add a **ة** and get **تَفْعِلَة**: **تَقْوِيَة**, *strengthening*.

So this lām is **strengthening** something. What does it strengthen? A **weak عَامِل**.

### 1.2 What Is an عَامِل?

Whenever something in a sentence is **manṣūb** or **majrūr**, we have to look for the **reason** — because the default state of any noun is **marfūʿ**. For a mafʿūl bih, the thing causing its naṣb is the **verb**. So the **عَامِل** in that case is the **fiʿl**.

Most of the time the ʿāmil is a verb; it could also be **إِنَّ** or some other word like that.

!!! important "Get the Terminology Right"
    - **عَامِل** — the one exerting the effect
    - **مَعمُول** — the one receiving it, the one that becomes manṣūb

Sometimes the ʿāmil is **weak** — it cannot exert its effect properly. There are **two reasons** this can happen.

### 1.3 Reason One — The ʿĀmil Comes After Its Maʿmūl

If the verb is **delayed** — the mafʿūl bih comes **before** it — the ʿāmil is weakened, and we **can** add a lām.

> **إِن كُنتُمْ لِلرُّؤْيَا تَعْبُرُونَ** (Yūsuf 43)
>
> *"…if you are able to interpret the dream."*

!!! example "The Analysis"
    The usual sequence — outside the Quran — would be **تَعْبُرُونَ الرُّؤْيَا**. There **تَعْبُرُونَ** is the verb and **الرُّؤْيَا** is the **mafʿūl bih**, manṣūb with an **implied** fatḥah: because it ends in an **alif**, and an alif is itself a vowel, the ḥarakah cannot show.

    In the āyah, the mafʿūl bih comes **before** the verb, so the lām is added: **لِلرُّؤْيَا**. It strengthens the effect of **تَعْبُرُونَ** upon **الرُّؤْيَا**. The ʿāmil is weak here *because it comes after* its maʿmūl.

!!! important "Not Compulsory"
    The first thing to understand is that this lām is **not compulsory**. The teacher asked for a Quranic example where the mafʿūl bih comes before the verb and there is **no** lām, and a student supplied one (the āyah is lost in the captions). That is why many books classify it as a **لَام زَائِدَة**.

### 1.4 What زَائِد Really Means

Extra in what sense? Does it mean it has no bearing on the meaning? **No.** It means that even if you omit it, **the sentence is still grammatically sound**.

!!! example "Sūrat Qāf (30)"
    > **يَوْمَ نَقُولُ لِجَهَنَّمَ هَلِ امْتَلَأْتِ وَتَقُولُ هَلْ مِن مَّزِيدٍ**
    >
    > *"The Day We will say to Jahannam, 'Are you full?' and it will say, 'Are there any more?'"*

    The **مِن** here is **زَائِدَة**. We can remove it — **هَلْ مَزِيدٌ** — and the sentence is still fine, with more or less the same meaning. That is the definition of a zāʾid word.

!!! important "Grammatically, Never Semantically"
    **زَائِد** is said speaking **grammatically**, never semantically. It does not mean the two sentences are 100% the same in meaning or effect. The same goes for lām al-taqwiyah, also sometimes called **لَام زَائِدَة**.

    *"When Allah is using a word and we label it as zāʾid, that does not mean it's extra or unnecessary. There is nothing unnecessary in the Quran."*

See [مِن الزِّيَادَة](../topics/min-ziyada.md).

### 1.5 Reason Two — The ʿĀmil Is a Derived Word

The second reason is that the ʿāmil is a **فَرْع** — a secondary worker. In easy words, it is one of the **derived words** (**المُشتَقَّات**): an **ism fāʿil**, for example.

> **إِنَّا نَحْنُ نَزَّلْنَا الذِّكْرَ وَإِنَّا لَهُ لَحَافِظُونَ** (al-Ḥijr 9)

!!! example "The Analysis"
    Allah is telling us this about the Quran. **حَافِظُونَ** is an **ism fāʿil** — not a verb. An ism fāʿil can take a mafʿūl bih, as the class has done before, but it is a **weak** ʿāmil because it is not a verb; its effect is not as strong. So we **can** add lām al-taqwiyah to strengthen it.

!!! summary "لَام التَّقوِيَة — The Two Reasons"
    | Reason | Meaning | Example |
    |---|---|---|
    | **تَأَخُّر العَامِل عَن مَعمُولِهِ** | The ʿāmil comes after its maʿmūl | **لِلرُّؤْيَا تَعْبُرُونَ** |
    | **كَون العَامِل فَرْعًا فِي العَمَل** | The ʿāmil is a derived word, not a verb | **لَهُ لَحَافِظُونَ** |

!!! tip "Why Know It If It's Optional?"
    Many ism fāʿil and similar words do the work of a verb — making something manṣūb, taking a mafʿūl bih — with **no** lām at all. It is **totally optional**. But when you meet it, you should be able to say what it is and why it is there: *it is lām al-taqwiyah, strengthening a weak ʿāmil, which here is a derived noun.*

!!! info "So What Difference Does It Make?"
    The subtle difference in meaning between the sentence with the lām and the sentence without it is delegated to **البَلَاغَة**. As far as grammar is concerned, both are the same and both are correct.

See [لَام التَّقوِيَة](../topics/lam-taqwiyah.md).

## 2. Āyah 12 — أَرْسِلْهُ مَعَنَا غَدًا

> **أَرْسِلْهُ مَعَنَا غَدًا يَرْتَعْ وَيَلْعَبْ وَإِنَّا لَهُ لَحَافِظُونَ**
>
> *"Send him with us tomorrow, that he may enjoy himself and play. And indeed, we will surely guard him."*

A student translated: *"Send him with us tomorrow…"* and was unsure of **يَرْتَعْ**, then continued: *"…to play… and certainly we will be protecting him."* **يَرْتَعْ** means to get refreshed — like when you go on a picnic and enjoy yourself and relax.

### 2.1 Do Not Confuse It with an Ordinary Lām

Before going further: many verbs take a **preposition** with them. The teacher used **ذَهَبَ**:

- **ذَهَبَ** is complete on its own; it does not need a mafʿūl bih.
- But we can extend it with a preposition: **ذَهَبَ إِلَى السُّوقِ** — now **السُّوق** is technically a **مَفعُول بِهِ غَيرُ صَرِيح** (an indirect object).
- **ذَهَبَ بِهِ** — *he went with him*, another indirect object.

Similarly, certain verbs take the **lām** as their **وَاسِطَة** (intermediary) to create such an object. **That lām is not lām al-taqwiyah.**

!!! important "The Omission Test"
    Lām al-taqwiyah is **زَائِدَة**, so you can rewrite the sentence without it. But can I rewrite **ذَهَبَ إِلَى السُّوقِ** as **ذَهَبَ السُّوقَ**? **I can't.** So the preposition there is not zāʾid. Likewise, if a verb usually takes a lām to create its object and you cannot omit it, it is **not** lām al-taqwiyah.

### 2.2 رَتَعَ — From the Book

!!! note "مَاشٍ and مَاشِيَة"
    **مَشَى** is *to walk*. The ism fāʿil is **مَاشٍ** — a pedestrian, a person who is walking. But **مَاشِيَة** is **cattle**, the ones who graze.

**رَتَعَتِ المَاشِيَةُ** — and **رَتْع** is the maṣdar — means the cattle **grazed however they wanted**.

!!! info "From Cattle to People"
    Initially the word was used for cattle grazing as they wish. Leave an animal in a meadow or field — a horse with no bridle, no reins, no halter; cows or goats in a field — and they can go here and there, eating wherever they like, *without any care in the world*. That is **رَتَعَ**.

    From there it came to be used for **humans**: going somewhere and spending carefree time, relaxing, **rejuvenating**. (The class settled on *relaxing* and *rejuvenate* as the best English.)

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **خَرَجْنَا فَرَتَعْنَا وَلَعِبْنَا** | We went out, and we relaxed and played |
| **مَرْتَع** | The place where you go to relax and rejuvenate — a picnic spot |

!!! question "Homework — Two Word Pairs"
    The teacher asked the class to look up the difference between **two pairs of words** that keep coming up in the Quran:

    1. Two words connected to **enjoyment, pleasures, and benefits**.
    2. The book glosses one word with another, *"but in the Quran these two words occur side by side. So that means there must be some difference — otherwise there is no point in bringing the same word beside another one."*

    The captions lost the words. The second pair is most likely **لَعِب** and **لَهْو**, glossed together here and discussed in [Session 10](session-10.md). See [لَهْو vs لَعِب](../topics/lahw-vs-laib.md).

See [رَتَعَ](../topics/verb-rataa.md).

### 2.3 حَفِظَ — To Guard

**حَفِظَ** means *to guard, to protect*. And from here we use the same word for **memorising the Quran**: we are, in a sense, guarding it.

!!! info "An Amazing Word"
    The word opens up so many things to think about.

    - **Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī** says that when you memorise the Quran, it is not so much you protecting the Quran; rather, **it is the Quran protecting you**. Memorising the Quran comes with a huge set of blessings.
    - And the aḥādīth tell us to guard the Quran, because it is **more prone to escape from your chests than a loosely tied camel**. We have to keep going over it, revising it, guarding it.

See [Root ح-ف-ظ](../topics/root-hafiza.md).

### 2.4 أَرْسَلَ — Why سَمَحَ لَهُ Is Not Lām al-Taqwiyah

**أَرْسَلَ** is **Form IV**: *to send*. A second verb with the same meaning is given. The book's gloss for **أَرْسِلْهُ مَعَنَا** is **اِسْمَحْ لَهُ بِالذَّهَابِ مَعَنَا** — *permit him to go with us*.

!!! question "Is the Lām in سَمَحَ لَهُ Lām al-Taqwiyah?"
    **No.** It is a normal prepositional lām, just like the **إِلَى** or **بِـ** after **ذَهَبَ**.

    **The key test:** neither of the two conditions is met. **لَهُ** does not come before the verb, and the ʿāmil is not a derivative. It is a normal verb in normal order, so it needs no strengthening.

    **سَمَحَ** does not take a direct mafʿūl bih, just like **ذَهَبَ** does not. When you want to extend the meaning, you use a preposition. So the **هُ** here is a **مَفعُول بِهِ غَيرُ صَرِيح**.

!!! important "The Two Tests"
    To decide whether a lām is lām al-taqwiyah, look for **these two conditions**. They decide it. And do not forget that lām al-taqwiyah is itself a preposition too — the prepositional lām simply has many different functions.

!!! note "A Promise for Next Class"
    The teacher recalled that the prepositional lām has *"around 30 or so — either 30 or 13, I have forgotten"* different meanings, and offered to go through a couple of them in every class. The students were keen, so **next class will take up some meanings of the lām**. See [لَام الجَار](../topics/lam-al-jar-meanings.md).

### 2.5 غَد — Tomorrow

**الغَد** is the day that comes **after** the day you are talking about, straight away, following on its **footsteps**. **عَقِب** is the heel or footstep; something coming on its heels comes right after it. (The teacher remembered a beautiful couplet here, could not recall it, and promised to share it in the group.)

!!! question "What Is the Original Form?"
    The book says the original form is **غَدْو**. What does it mean that the **lām** has been omitted? A student first thought of an **ال** at the beginning — no. In the template **فَعَل**, the **ل** stands for the **third radical** (**لَام الكَلِمَة**). So the **third radical**, the **و**, has been dropped: **غَدْو → غَد**, and then the tanwīn: **غَدٌ**.

!!! example "The Original Form Survives in Poetry"
    > **إِنَّ مَعَ اليَوْمِ أَخَاهُ غَدْوَا**
    >
    > *"Indeed, with the day is its brother, tomorrow."*

    It is a beautiful line: with every day there is always its brother, tomorrow. Just as we say in English, *tomorrow is another day*, or *there is always a tomorrow*.

See [غَد](../topics/ghad-tomorrow.md).

### 2.6 مَعَ vs عِنْدَ

**مَعَ** is a **ظَرْف** — an adverb of place or time. The book gives it adverbially: **خَرَجْنَا مَعًا** — *we left together*.

!!! important "The Distinction"
    | Sentence | Implication |
    |---|---|
    | **كُنْتُ عِنْدَ مُحَمَّدٍ** | *I was by Muḥammad* — suggests you **went to a place where Muḥammad already was**: his house, his office |
    | **كُنْتُ مَعَ مُحَمَّدٍ** | *I was with Muḥammad* — points **only to the company**, with no indication of where it happened: his house, your house, or out somewhere |

See [مَعَ vs عِنْدَ](../topics/maa-vs-inda.md).

### 2.7 The Hamzah of Form IV and the Imperative

The root letters are **ر-س-ل**, and the Form IV **māḍī** adds a hamzah: **أَرْسَلَ**.

!!! important "Where the Hamzah Goes"
    - The hamzah is part of the māḍī. In the **muḍāriʿ** it is **dropped**: behind the scenes it is **يُؤَرْسِلُ**, but it becomes **يُرْسِلُ**.
    - It **stays** in three forms: the **māḍī**, the **amr**, and the **maṣdar**.

!!! example "Building أَرْسِلْ"
    The book's point: the **أَمْر** is formed from the **original** muḍāriʿ, **تُؤَرْسِلُ** (*you…*).

    1. Drop the muḍāriʿ prefix: **ءَرْسِلُ**
    2. Give the last letter a sukūn: **أَرْسِلْ**
    3. It does **not** start with a sukūn, it starts with a vowelled hamzah — so no helping **hamzat al-waṣl** is needed.

    This is exactly why the Form IV imperative has a hamzah while the muḍāriʿ does not.

!!! note "Reading the Book's Verb"
    The book says the amr **is formed** from the original. The verb is most likely **يُصَاغُ** (the captions lost it), which fits the teacher's walk-through: a **majhūl** muḍāriʿ. Just as **يَكْتُبُ** becomes **يُكْتَبُ** — ḍammah on the prefix, fatḥah on the second radical — here the second radical is a **weak letter**, so it becomes an alif.

    From the same root comes **صِيغَة** — what is formed, a pattern — as in **صِيغَة المُبَالَغَة**.

### 2.8 Iʿrāb — مَعَنَا غَدًا يَرْتَعْ وَيَلْعَبْ

- **مَعَ** and **غَدًا** are both **ظَرْف**, and that is why both are **manṣūb**. **مَعَ** tells you the company — with whom he is sent — and **غَدًا** gives the time. *Send him with us tomorrow.*
- **يَرْتَعْ** is **majzūm** in reply to the **طَلَب**: when a command comes first, the muḍāriʿ after it is majzūm. **يَلْعَبْ** follows it. The class has covered this before.

### 2.9 وَإِنَّا لَهُ لَحَافِظُونَ

The dictionary shows **حَفِظَ** taking a **direct** mafʿūl bih, unlike **سَمَحَ**, which needs a preposition. So why the lām in **لَهُ**?

Because the **هُ** is the mafʿūl bih of **حَافِظُونَ**, and it comes **before** its ʿāmil. The ʿāmil is weak, so the **حَرف جَرّ زَائِد**, the **lām**, strengthens it.

## 3. The Book's Own Sentence — Asking About Every Jumlah

The book defines the lām as coming to strengthen an ʿāmil **ضَعُفَ عَنِ العَمَلِ** — *that has become too weak to work*.

!!! example "A Student Analyses It"
    - **ضَعُفَ** — a **fiʿl māḍī**, with its fāʿil a hidden pronoun.
    - What is this sentence doing **inside the other sentence**? It is nested. It does not start the speech.
    - **Rule:** a jumlah after a **definite** noun is a **حَال**; after an **indefinite** noun it is a **نَعت**. **عَامِل** is indefinite, so **ضَعُفَ عَنِ العَمَلِ** is a **naʿt** for it, giving more information about it.

!!! tip "Where You Are Now in Your Arabic"
    *"I want you to start paying more and more attention to the iʿrāb of any jumlah or shibh jumlah that you come across. Start asking: what is this jumlah doing here?"*

    When a sentence is nested inside another, it has some iʿrāb attached to it. Train your mind to find that hidden iʿrāb. But a sentence that **starts the speech** is a **جُملَة اِستِئنَافِيَّة** — **لَا مَحَلَّ لَهَا مِنَ الإِعرَاب**, *it has no place in iʿrāb*, because it is completely new and not inside any other sentence.

See [Embedded Sentences](../topics/jumla-embedded.md).

## 4. The Book's Examples of Lām al-Taqwiyah

### 4.1 Reason One — لِرَبِّهِمْ يَرْهَبُونَ

The weakness comes either from **تَأَخُّر العَامِل عَن مَعمُولِهِ**. (**تَأَخَّرَ** is Form V, like **تَكَلَّمَ**: *to be delayed*.)

> **وَلَمَّا سَكَتَ عَن مُّوسَى الْغَضَبُ أَخَذَ الْأَلْوَاحَ ۖ وَفِي نُسْخَتِهَا هُدًى وَرَحْمَةٌ لِّلَّذِينَ هُمْ لِرَبِّهِمْ يَرْهَبُونَ** (al-Aʿrāf 154)
>
> *"And when the anger subsided from Mūsā, he took up the tablets; and in their inscription was guidance and mercy for those who hold their Lord in awe."*

!!! info "The Story Behind It"
    Mūsā عليه السلام came back from **Ṭūr** and found that the people behind him had started worshipping the **golden calf**. He was so angry that he dropped the **slates** on which the Torah was written.

    The Quran came down as an **oral** transmission: Jibrīl عليه السلام used to come and recite it. That also happened with Mūsā عليه السلام, but the first bulk of the Torah that came to him on Ṭūr, as many traditions tell us, was **already written down on stone slates that came down from heaven**. Some say that because Mūsā عليه السلام could read and write, he himself wrote down whatever waḥy came to him. Either way, he had these slates in his hands and dropped them in his anger.

    **سَكَتَ** here means *to go away, to leave*: when the anger left him, he picked the slates up again. And within their writing was guidance and mercy.

!!! example "The Lām"
    **رَهِبَ** — *to have awe of something* — normally takes a mafʿūl bih straight after it: it could have been **يَرْهَبُونَ رَبَّهُمْ**. Because **رَبَّهُمْ** comes **before** the verb, the ʿāmil has become weak, and the lām is used: **لِرَبِّهِمْ يَرْهَبُونَ**.

The book's second example is the one the class already did: **إِن كُنتُمْ لِلرُّؤْيَا تَعْبُرُونَ**. Normally **الرُّؤْيَا** comes directly after the verb with no lām; when the ʿāmil is delayed, its effect weakens and the lām enters.

See [The Tablets of Mūsā](../topics/alwah-musa.md).

### 4.2 Reason Two — Two Reasons Gathered in لَهُ لَحَافِظُونَ

The second reason, as on the whiteboard: the ʿāmil is **فَرْع**. The verb is the primary worker, and it always makes its object manṣūb. For a word like an ism fāʿil, that is not its primary job.

!!! important "Two Reasons Together"
    In **وَإِنَّا لَهُ لَحَافِظُونَ** you could also write **حَافِظُوهُ** — grammatically acceptable, because an ism fāʿil can take a direct mafʿūl bih — yet the lām has been used, and here **two reasons have gathered**:

    1. **حَافِظُون** is not a verb; it is the plural of an **ism fāʿil**.
    2. The **هُ** is coming **before** it.

### 4.3 Lām al-Taqwiyah vs اللَّام المُزَحلَقَة

!!! question "What Kind of Lām Is the One in لَحَافِظُونَ?"
    A student answered: **مُزَحلَقَة**. *"Very good. You should be able to tell all sorts of lāms apart."*

A student asked whether there is a difference between the two. **Definitely.** The muzaḥlaqah comes because of **إِنَّ**.

!!! example "How the Lām Got There"
    1. **حَامِدٌ مَرِيضٌ** — *Ḥāmid is sick.* No emphasis at all.
    2. **لَحَامِدٌ مَرِيضٌ** — now I am in earnest, and put some emphasis in my speech. This is **لَام الاِبتِدَاء**, at the beginning of a sentence for the sake of emphasis.
    3. **إِنَّ حَامِدًا مَرِيضٌ** — another tool of emphasis.
    4. **إِنَّ حَامِدًا لَمَرِيضٌ** — combine the two. We cannot have both at the beginning, so we **push the lām** to the khabar. Now we have **double emphasis**.

!!! important "Reserved for a Reason"
    This very high form of emphasis is usually only reserved for when **somebody is denying the fact**. You don't start your speech like that for no reason. Similarly, an **oath** is a tool of emphasis: you only take an oath if somebody is denying you.

    So the brothers are **vehemently** insisting that they are definitely going to guard him. Why so vehement? As discussed last time: either to hide their own guilt, or because they could already sense that Yaʿqūb عليه السلام wasn't trusting them with his younger son.

So **لَهُ** is lām al-taqwiyah, attached to the mafʿūl bih of **حَافِظُونَ**, and the lām of **لَحَافِظُونَ** is the muzaḥlaqah. See [اللام المزحلقة](../topics/lam-muzaalaqa.md).

### 4.4 With a Maṣdar

When a **maṣdar** has a mafʿūl bih, you can also use the lām on it.

> **بَدَأَتْ دِرَاسَتِي لِلْعُلُومِ الإِسْلَامِيَّةِ فِي الجَامِعَةِ**
>
> *My study of the Islamic sciences started at the university level.*

| Word | Iʿrāb |
|---|---|
| **بَدَأَتْ** | Fiʿl |
| **دِرَاسَتِي** | Fāʿil — **دِرَاسَة** is the **maṣdar** of *to study* |
| **ـِي** | Muḍāf ilayh |
| **لِلْعُلُومِ** | **العُلُوم** is the **mafʿūl bih** of **دِرَاسَة**: *my study of what?* |
| **الإِسْلَامِيَّةِ** | **نَعت** for **العُلُوم** |
| **فِي الجَامِعَةِ** | **مُتَعَلِّق** — connected with the verb |

!!! important "How to Analyse a Zāʾid Word"
    I could rewrite the sentence without the lām: **دِرَاسَتِي العُلُومَ الإِسْلَامِيَّةَ** — there **العُلُومَ** is plainly manṣūb. With the lām, we say it is **مَجرُور لَفْظًا، مَنصُوب مَحَلًّا**: majrūr in its wording, but by function **manṣūb**, because it is the mafʿūl bih of **دِرَاسَة**.

    The same analysis applies to **هَلْ مِن مَّزِيدٍ**: without **مِن** the word **مَزِيد** would be marfūʿ, so we say it is **majrūr in wording, marfūʿ in position**. Remove the zāʾid word, and see what remains.

See [The Maṣdar's Muḍāf](../topics/masdar-mudaf.md).

### 4.5 The Lām Is Not Compulsory — Three Examples

The book mentions this at the very end, but the teacher highlighted it as the first thing to remember: **the lām is not compulsory**. It is permissible to leave it out entirely.

!!! example "زِيَارَتِي لَهُ / زِيَارَتِي إِيَّاهُ"
    **زِيَارَة** is a **maṣdar**: *my visiting him*. **هُ** is its **mafʿūl bih**. And because it is a pronoun, it cannot show iʿrāb.

    It is equally permissible to say **زِيَارَتِي إِيَّاهُ**, leaving the lām out of the equation. Why bring in **إِيَّا**? Because the pronoun cannot stand on its own: it is an attached one, and **زِيَارَتِي** already has an attached pronoun.

    If there were a person's name, it is simple: **زِيَارَتِي مُحَمَّدًا** or **زِيَارَتِي لِمُحَمَّدٍ**. Both are permissible.

See [إيّا](../topics/iyyaa-pronouns.md).

!!! example "Sūrat Maryam (2) — A Maṣdar Without the Lām"
    > **ذِكْرُ رَحْمَتِ رَبِّكَ عَبْدَهُ زَكَرِيَّا**
    >
    > *"[This is] a mention of the mercy of your Lord to His servant Zakariyyā."*

    - This is the second āyah of Sūrat Maryam; before it there is only **كٓهيعٓصٓ**. The **mubtadaʾ is omitted**: *this*, whatever Allah is saying in the sūrah, is a **ذِكْر**.
    - **ذِكْرُ** is a noun and a **muḍāf**; **رَحْمَتِ** is its **muḍāf ilayh** and a muḍāf itself; then **رَبِّكَ**. So we have a chain.
    - **عَبْدَهُ** is **manṣūb**: it is the direct **mafʿūl bih** of the maṣdar **رَحْمَة**. *The mention of the mercy of your Lord on His servant.* No lām — it is not compulsory.

!!! example "Sūrat Ibrāhīm (47) — An Ism Fāʿil Without the Lām"
    > **فَلَا تَحْسَبَنَّ اللَّهَ مُخْلِفَ وَعْدِهِ رُسُلَهُ ۗ إِنَّ اللَّهَ عَزِيزٌ ذُو انتِقَامٍ**
    >
    > *"So do not think that Allah will fail His promise to His messengers. Indeed, Allah is Mighty, the Possessor of retribution."*

    - **تَحْسَبَنَّ** has the heavy **نُون التَّوكِيد**: *do not dare think or assume*.
    - **حَسِبَ** takes **two mafʿūl**: **اللَّهَ** is number one and **مُخْلِفَ** is number two.
    - **مُخْلِف** is an **ism fāʿil** from Form IV **أَخْلَفَ**: to say something and then do something else. The **مُخْلِف** is the one going back on his word; **وَعْد** is the thing he goes back on.
    - **رُسُلَهُ** is **manṣūb**, as the mafʿūl of **مُخْلِف**. And as you can see, there is no lām.
    - **عَزِيز** — Mighty, powerful; **ذُو انتِقَامٍ** — the Possessor of retribution.

!!! important "Adjusting Our Terminology for Allah's Name"
    **اللَّهَ** is manṣūb, but we hesitate to call it a plain mafʿūl bih: it feels disrespectful with the name of Allah, so the teacher recalled that a respectful expression is used instead (*"I think something like that"*).

    Likewise, when we make duʿāʾ we do not call the verb a **فِعل أَمر**, because we cannot command Allah. We say **فِعل دُعَاء**. When Allah's name is involved, we adjust our terminology to show respect.

See [Arabic Grammar Methodology](../topics/arabic-grammar-methodology.md).

### 4.6 A Student's Question — Which Lām Is This?

!!! question "Is This a Different Lām from the Lām Meaning *For*?"
    A student noted that we can't translate the lām in these cases as *for*. So is it a different lām?

    The label **حَرف جَرّ** is not concerned with the meaning. It still gives **jarr** to what follows, as **لِلْعُلُومِ** shows. A ḥarf jarr like the lām has many functions. One is **تَقْوِيَة**; another is the usual *for*, the most widespread meaning. Whatever the difference in meaning, it is still called a **ḥarf jarr**, because it still makes its noun majrūr.

    Going over the different meanings of the lām next class should make this clearer, in shāʾ Allāh.

## 5. Āyah 13 — إِنِّي لَيَحْزُنُنِي

> **قَالَ إِنِّي لَيَحْزُنُنِي أَن تَذْهَبُوا بِهِ وَأَخَافُ أَن يَأْكُلَهُ الذِّئْبُ وَأَنتُمْ عَنْهُ غَافِلُونَ**
>
> *"He said, 'Indeed, it saddens me that you should take him, and I fear that a wolf would eat him while you are heedless of him.'"*

Yaʿqūb عليه السلام replies, and once again there is emphasis. A student translated well: *"He said: indeed, it makes me sad that you go with him, and I am afraid that a wolf will eat him and you are heedless of him."*

!!! tip "Taking a Step Up"
    The class's translations are now pretty good, so the teacher wants to start analysing at least some bits of each āyah:

    - **وَأَنتُمْ عَنْهُ غَافِلُونَ**: what kind of **وَ** is this? *While you are heedless* — it is the **وَاو** of the **حَال**.
    - The whole of what follows **قَالَ** is called **مَقُول القَوْل** — quotation, what he said.

### 5.1 حَزِنَ and حَزَنَ

There are two **bābs** here. The teacher named the well-known bāb instead of *"relying on the printing or misprinting of the tashkīl marks"*.

| Verb | Bāb | Meaning | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| **حَزِنَ / يَحْزَنُ** | **سَمِعَ** (kasrah on the second radical; fatḥah in the muḍāriʿ) | To **be sad** | **لَازِم** — intransitive |
| **حَزَنَ / يَحْزُنُ** | **نَصَرَ** | To **make someone sad** | **مُتَعَدٍّ** — transitive |

**حُزْن** is the opposite of happiness (**فَرَح**). The one who is sad is **حَزِين**.

!!! example "لَا تَحْزَنْ — Sūrat al-Tawbah (40)"
    > **إِذْ يَقُولُ لِصَاحِبِهِ لَا تَحْزَنْ إِنَّ اللَّهَ مَعَنَا**
    >
    > *"When he said to his companion, 'Do not be sad; indeed, Allah is with us.'"*

    The teacher asked who said this to whom and where. A student offered **Abū Bakr**, and the setting is the **cave of Thawr**: the āyah has the Prophet ﷺ saying it to his companion Abū Bakr رضي الله عنه. The muḍāriʿ has a fatḥah on the second radical, so this is the meaning *to be sad*.

!!! note "A Book by That Name"
    The teacher remembered a very famous book, ***Lā Taḥzan*** (*Don't Be Sad*), by an author *"whose name ended in Qarnī"*, translated into English a few years ago.

!!! example "لَا يَحْزُنْكَ — Sūrat Luqmān (23)"
    > **وَمَن كَفَرَ فَلَا يَحْزُنكَ كُفْرُهُ ۚ إِلَيْنَا مَرْجِعُهُمْ فَنُنَبِّئُهُم بِمَا عَمِلُوا ۚ إِنَّ اللَّهَ عَلِيمٌ بِذَاتِ الصُّدُورِ**
    >
    > *"And whoever disbelieves, let not his disbelief sadden you. To Us is their return, and We will inform them of what they did. Indeed, Allah is All-Knowing of what is within the breasts."*

    Here **كَ** is the mafʿūl bih and **كُفْرُهُ** is the fāʿil: his disbelief should not make **you** sad. The ḍammah on the second radical signals the transitive meaning.

See [حَزِنَ / حَزَنَ](../topics/verb-hazana.md) and [Verb Families](../topics/verb-babs.md).

### 5.2 ذَهَبَ — Made Transitive

**ذَهَبَ** is *to go*, and the book says it is **made transitive** — **يُعَدَّى** — with a particle or with a hamzah. (Without planning it, this is exactly what the class discussed with **ذَهَبَ** earlier.)

!!! note "يُعَدَّى"
    What kind of word is it? **Majhūl.** The māḍī has four letters (**عَدَّى**), so even in the passive the prefix takes a ḍammah.

!!! important "Two Ways to Make ذَهَبَ Transitive"
    **1. With a preposition — بِـ.** In this very āyah, **أَن تَذْهَبُوا بِهِ** — *that you take him away with you*. The **هُ** is a mafʿūl bih (indirect). **ذَهَبَ** cannot be transitive on its own; it is **لَازِم**.

    **2. With a hamzah — Form IV.** Many, many verbs become transitive when you bring them to Form IV: **أَذْهَبَ** — *to take away*.

!!! example "Sūrat Fāṭir (34)"
    > **وَقَالُوا الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي أَذْهَبَ عَنَّا الْحَزَنَ ۖ إِنَّ رَبَّنَا لَغَفُورٌ شَكُورٌ**
    >
    > *"And they will say, 'All praise belongs to Allah, who has taken away grief from us. Indeed, our Lord is Forgiving and Appreciative.'"*

See [Intransitive and Transitive Verbs](../topics/lazim-mutaaddi.md).

### 5.3 شَكُور Said of Allah

!!! important "Not Grateful — Gracious"
    When we use **شَكُور** for Allah, it does not mean being *grateful*.

    Think of being in front of a personality extremely great in status, a president or a king. You bring a gift that is not up to their standard, but they are gracious enough to accept it, and they thank you for it. You would not say they are *grateful*; you would say they are **gracious**.

    So **شُكْر** used for people means **grateful**. Used for Allah it means **gracious**: He appreciates whatever you are doing and is gracious enough to accept it. We can't use the word *gratitude* for Him.

See [شَكُور](../topics/sighat-al-mubalaghah.md).

### 5.4 خَافَ

**خَافَ / يَخَافُ** has several maṣdars listed by the book. The class identified the last one, on the pattern with a **مِيم**, as a **مَصدَر مِيمِي** (**مَخَافَة**).

!!! info "The Definition of خَوْف"
    As discussed before, **خَوْف** has a broader meaning than simply *being scared*. It is being wary of, or **anticipating**:

    1. **Something disliked** (**مَكرُوه**) — fearing that something unpleasant might happen; or
    2. **The loss of something beloved** (**فَوَات مَحبُوب**) — fearing that something good you love might go away. **فَوَات** is the maṣdar of *to miss, to go away*.

The person who fears is **خَائِف**. **خَافَ** is transitive in itself; it needs no preposition.

!!! example "Form IV — أَخَافَ and مُخِيف"
    Bring it to **Form IV**, **أَخَافَ**, and it means *to make someone scared*. The ism fāʿil follows the Form IV pattern (**مُفْعِل**):

    - **Original:** **مُخْوِف**, with a kasrah on the wāw.
    - But **wāw and kasrah are not compatible** in Arabic, so *"some shifting and sliding of things happens"* and it becomes **مُخِيف**.

!!! example "Sūrat Hūd (103)"
    > **إِنَّ فِي ذَٰلِكَ لَآيَةً لِّمَنْ خَافَ عَذَابَ الْآخِرَةِ ۚ ذَٰلِكَ يَوْمٌ مَّجْمُوعٌ لَّهُ النَّاسُ**
    >
    > *"Indeed in that is a sign for the one who fears the punishment of the Hereafter. That is a Day for which the people will be gathered."*

    The teacher asked what kind of lām is in **لَّهُ**, gave a tentative answer (lost in the captions), and held back: *"Let me check, because it's Quran, so I don't want to make any mistakes."* The teacher will **come back to it in the group**.

See [خَوْف](../topics/khawf.md) and [Phonetic Harmony](../topics/phonetic-harmony.md).

## 8. Key Lessons from This Session

!!! tip "Summary of Lessons"
    1. **لَام التَّقوِيَة** strengthens a **weak عَامِل**, and an ʿāmil is weak for one of two reasons: it comes **after its maʿmūl**, or it is a **derived word** (**فَرْع**), not a verb.
    2. It is **always optional**. Its absence is not an error; its presence should still be identifiable.
    3. **زَائِد** means removable **grammatically, never semantically**. There is nothing unnecessary in the Quran. The nuance belongs to **البَلَاغَة**.
    4. A lām that a verb needs to reach its object, like **سَمَحَ لَهُ**, is an **ordinary preposition**. Apply the **two conditions** to tell them apart.
    5. **رَتَعَ** began with cattle grazing freely and moved to people relaxing — Arabic moving once again from the physical to the wider meaning.
    6. **حِفْظ** of the Quran is guarding it. In al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī's words, it is really the Quran protecting you; and it must be kept up, or it escapes faster than a loosely tied camel.
    7. **غَد** is originally **غَدْو** with its third radical dropped; poetry preserves the original.
    8. **عِنْدَ** suggests you went to where someone already was; **مَعَ** indicates company alone.
    9. Form IV's hamzah drops in the muḍāriʿ but stays in the māḍī, amr, and maṣdar. The amr needs no hamzat al-waṣl because the original form starts with a vowelled hamzah.
    10. **Ask for the iʿrāb of every nested jumlah**: after a definite noun it is usually a ḥāl, after an indefinite noun a naʿt; a sentence starting the speech has no iʿrāb position.
    11. **لَهُ لَحَافِظُونَ** has two reasons gathered for lām al-taqwiyah, followed by the muzaḥlaqah — two different lāms side by side.
    12. Heavy emphasis is reserved for **denial**, which makes the brothers' vehemence telling.
    13. A word after a zāʾid particle is analysed as **مَجرُور لَفْظًا** and whatever it would be without the particle **مَحَلًّا**.
    14. Maṣdars and ism fāʿil can take a **direct mafʿūl** with no lām: **عَبْدَهُ** (Maryam 2), **رُسُلَهُ** (Ibrāhīm 47).
    15. We **adjust our grammatical terminology** out of respect for Allah: **فِعل دُعَاء**, not fiʿl amr.
    16. **حَزِنَ / يَحْزَنُ** is intransitive (*to be sad*); **حَزَنَ / يَحْزُنُ** is transitive (*to sadden*).
    17. **ذَهَبَ** is made transitive with **بِـ** or with a Form IV hamzah (**أَذْهَبَ**).
    18. **شَكُور** of Allah means **gracious**, not grateful.
    19. **خَوْف** is anticipating something disliked or the loss of something loved.
    20. Knowing every word does not guarantee understanding a sentence. Comprehension comes with rereading, and it is the same for teachers too.
