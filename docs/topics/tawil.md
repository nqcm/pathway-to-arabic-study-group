# تَأْوِيل — Interpretation as "Returning"

**تَأْوِيل** is the **maṣdar of Form II** from the root **أ-و-ل**, whose Form I verb **آلَ / يَؤُولُ** means *to return to something*. Interpretation, in Arabic, is literally a *returning*.

---

## The Derivational Family

| Form | Verb | Maṣdar | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | **آلَ / يَؤُولُ** | **أَوْل / مَآل** | To return (to) |
| II | **أَوَّلَ / يُؤَوِّلُ** | **تَأْوِيل** | To return something → to explain, interpret it |

**أَوَّل** — *first* — belongs to the same root. What is *first* is what things return to.

---

## The Arabic Definition

> **تَفْسِيرُ مَا يَؤُولُ إِلَيْهِ الشَّيْء**
>
> *"The explanation of that to which a thing returns."*

!!! example "Why 'Returning' Means 'Explaining'"
    Take a concept **A** and produce an explanation of it. What is that explanation doing? It is **bringing the meaning back to A**. An explanation is precisely the thing that returns you to the original's meaning — which is how the root of *returning* became the root of *interpreting*.

!!! tip "Check Machine Translation Against Your Grammar"
    Asked to render **تَفْسِيرُ مَا يَؤُولُ إِلَيْهِ الشَّيْء**, Google Translate produced *"interpreting what leads to creation"* — which the teacher rejected on the spot: there is no **خَلْق** anywhere in the phrase. The correct sense is *"interpretation of what it leads/returns to."* A useful live reminder that machine translation of technical Arabic must be checked against the grammar you already know.

## Examples from the Quran

> **وَيُعَلِّمُكَ مِن تَأْوِيلِ الْأَحَادِيثِ** — *"…and He will teach you from the interpretation of narrations."* (Yūsuf 12:6)

The phrase **تَأْوِيل الأَحَادِيث** is the subject of a range of tafsīr opinions:

| # | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Interpretation of dreams** — the majority reading |
| 2 | The knowledge of **explaining knowledge** — taking difficult matters and making them clear |
| 3 | **الحِكْمَة** — wisdom, and the ability to explain wisdom to people |
| 4 | Narrations of **previous generations** and the previous books |
| 5 | The **proofs of tawḥīd** |
| 6 | The **previous scriptures** and the sunan of those before |

!!! important "Why the Majority Reading Prevails"
    Most mufassirūn combine the first reading with the phrase's larger sense: *Allah will choose you for prophethood and teach you from this special knowledge — the interpretation of dreams.* The sūrah's own narrative confirms it: Yūsuf عليه السلام later interprets the dreams of his fellow prisoners and then the dream of the king. Just as every prophet was given a particular gift as his speciality, this was the speciality of Yūsuf عليه السلام.

Note also the **مِن** in **مِن تَأْوِيلِ الْأَحَادِيثِ** — it is **تَبعِيضِيَّة**, *some of*; see [[min-tabidiyya]]. And on the word **أَحَادِيث** itself, see [[root-hadatha]].
