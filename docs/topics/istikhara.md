# اِسْتِخَارَة — Seeking Goodness from Allah

**اِسْتِخَارَة** is the maṣdar of **اِسْتَخَارَ / يَسْتَخِير**, a **Form X** verb from the root **خ-ي-ر** (*khayr*, good). Its morphology alone tells you what it is: **seeking goodness**.

---

## The Morphology Settles the Meaning

| Arabic | Analysis |
|---|---|
| **خَيْر** | The root meaning — *good, goodness* |
| **اِسْتَخَارَ / يَسْتَخِير** | Form X verb |
| **اِسْتِخَارَة** | Maṣdar, Form X |

!!! important "Form X Means *Seeking*"
    Compare **اِسْتَغْفَرَ** — Form X of **غَفَرَ** (*to forgive*) — which does not mean "to forgive" but **to seek forgiveness**. By exactly the same construction, **اِسْتِخَارَة** means **seeking khayr from Allah**. Nothing more mysterious than that.

    This is a case where knowing the verb form is not an academic exercise: the pattern by itself dismantles a large body of folk belief.

---

## What Istikhāra Is Not

!!! warning "The Superstitions"
    A great deal of superstition has attached itself to istikhāra — particularly widespread in the Indo-Pak subcontinent — treating it as a kind of magical procedure with fixed protocols:

    - that it must be prayed immediately before sleeping;
    - that one must dress a certain way;
    - that particular **signs must be seen in a dream**, and only then has the istikhāra "worked";
    - and that once the right signs appear, whatever is done afterwards **cannot go wrong** — the job cannot turn out toxic, the marriage cannot fail.

    The teacher's assessment of this was blunt: *"sheer nonsense."*

Istikhāra is simply asking your Lord for goodness, asking for His guidance, and asking Him to put **خَيْر** into whatever you are about to undertake.

---

## It Does Not Replace مَشْوَرَة

Consulting Allah does not remove the obligation to consult people and do your own work. **مَشْوَرَة** — mutual consultation — carries great weight in the Quran and is not displaced by istikhāra.

!!! example "Due Diligence Alongside Duʿāʾ"
    Someone weighing a marriage proposal still investigates the person, asks those who know them, and does their due diligence. Someone taking a new job still researches the role and the market, and asks specialists for their recommendations.

    Istikhāra runs **alongside** all of this — before it, during it, and after it — as one settles toward a decision.

---

## The X-Axis and the Y-Axis

!!! example "Why a 'Failed' Outcome Is Not a Failed Istikhāra"
    Picture a point on a plain number line — the **x-axis** alone. It may be moving forward, moving backward, or sitting perfectly still. If it is still, you conclude nothing is progressing.

    But that judgement holds only because you are looking at one axis. Add the **y-axis** and the very same point that is stationary, or even sliding backward on x, may be climbing steeply on y.

    **Bringing Allah into a decision is adding that second dimension.** A decision made with istikhāra may still cost materially: the business may lose money, the marriage may prove difficult. But what is lost on the x-axis is being gained on the y-axis — because the endeavour was entered asking Allah to place khayr in it, and He does.

---

## The Short Duʿāʾ

Even with no time to pray the two rakʿahs, the istikhāra is not lost:

> **اللَّهُمَّ خِرْ لِي وَاخْتَرْ لِي**
>
> *"O Allah, choose good for me and choose for me."*

!!! tip "Make It a Habit for Small Decisions"
    Do not reserve istikhāra for life-altering decisions like marriage. Get into the habit of consulting Allah and asking for khayr before **anything** — small, ordinary, everyday decisions included.
