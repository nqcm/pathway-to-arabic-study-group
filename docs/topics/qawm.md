# قَوْم — Originally a Group of Men

**قَوْم** today means *a people* or *a nation*, including both men and women. Its **original** meaning, however, was **a group of men, without women** — and the Quran and classical poetry preserve that sense.

---

## Meaning and Origin

!!! info "Why Men Are Called قَوْم"
    **قَوْم** carries the meaning of **قِيَام** — *standing* (to a task). Men were called **قَوْم** because they were responsible for the important and difficult matters: war, trade, and so on. This definition comes from a very old, excellent dictionary.

!!! note "No Singular of Its Own"
    **قَوْم** is grammatically singular but always carries a **plural** meaning. There is no singular from the same word; a different word is used to refer to one.

---

## Examples from the Quran

> **يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا لَا يَسْخَرْ قَوْمٌ مِّن قَوْمٍ عَسَىٰ أَن يَكُونُوا خَيْرًا مِّنْهُمْ وَلَا نِسَاءٌ مِّن نِّسَاءٍ عَسَىٰ أَن يَكُنَّ خَيْرًا مِّنْهُنَّ** (al-Ḥujurāt 11)

**قَوْم** stands opposite **نِسَاء** — so here it means **men**. (This does not mean men may mock women or vice versa; it is simply how Allah has phrased it.)

> **اقْتُلُوا يُوسُفَ أَوِ اطْرَحُوهُ أَرْضًا يَخْلُ لَكُمْ وَجْهُ أَبِيكُمْ وَتَكُونُوا مِن بَعْدِهِ قَوْمًا صَالِحِينَ** (Yūsuf 9)

## From Poetry — Zuhayr's Taunt

> **وَمَا أَدْرِي وَسَوْفَ إِخَالُ أَدْرِي** ⁂ **أَقَوْمٌ آلُ حِصْنٍ أَمْ نِسَاءُ**
>
> *"I do not know — and I don't think I will ever know — are the people of Ḥiṣn men, or women?"*

**حِصْن** is a fort (cf. the book **حِصْنُ المُسْلِم**). The line taunts an enemy hiding in their fort instead of fighting. Again **قَوْم** contrasts with **نِسَاء**. The teacher attributed the line to **Zuhayr ibn Abī Sulmā**, unsure of the poem. On **إِخَالُ**, see [إِخَالُ — The Banū Asad Kasrah](ikhalu-banu-asad.md).
