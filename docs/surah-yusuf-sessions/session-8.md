# Surah Yusuf — Study Session 8

## Overview

The main topics covered in this session are:

- **Āyah 6 in full** — Yaʿqūb عليه السلام completes his reply to Yūsuf: selection, teaching, and the completion of Allah's favour
- **اِجتَبَى / يَجتَبِي** (Form VIII) — "to choose"; its relative **اِصطَفَى / المُصطَفَى**, and a long practical discussion of **اِستِخَارَة**: what it really is, the superstitions attached to it, its relationship to **مَشوَرَة**, and the short duʿāʾ for it
- **الاِسم المَنقُوص** — why **مُجتَبٍ** ends in two kasrahs, the three situations in which the dropped **يَاء** returns, and the contrasting **الاِسم المَقصُور** **مُجتَبًى / المُجتَبَى**
- **تَأْوِيل** — the Form II maṣdar of **آلَ يَؤُولُ** ("to return"), and why "explanation" and "returning" share a root
- **الأَحَادِيث** — **حَدِيث** as *event*, as *new*, and as *speech*; its **سَمَاعِي** plural; and the Quran calling itself **حَدِيث**
- The **six tafsīr opinions** on **تَأْوِيل الأَحَادِيث**, and why the majority read it as dream interpretation
- **يُعَلِّم / تَعَلَّمَ** and **تَمَّ / أَتَمَّ** — Form II, IV and V meanings worked out from Form I
- **نِعمَة** defined in Arabic, its two plurals, and **أَنعَمَ عَلَيْهِ**
- **Stacking attached pronouns** — when two ḍamīrs may sit on one verb, and when **إِيَّا** must be brought in (**إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ**)
- **آل** and **أَبَوَيْكَ** — the "two fathers" who are in fact grandfather and great-grandfather
- **كَذَٰلِكَ** as a **نَائِب عَن المَفْعُول المُطْلَق**, its **مَحَلًّا** iʿrāb, and the anatomy of **ذَٰلِكَ** (**ذَا + لَام البُعْد + كَاف الخِطَاب**)
- **مِن التَّبعِيضِيَّة** and **مَا المَصدَرِيَّة** in **كَمَا أَتَمَّهَا**
- **قَبْلُ** and **بَعْدُ** — the full three-case rule with Quranic and poetic evidence
- **جَمْع مُنتَهَى الجُمُوع** and **مَمنُوع مِن الصَّرْف** — two couplets from the **أَلفِيَّة**, the word **رَدِيف**, and **إِبرَاهِيم** as a **بَدَل**
- **صِيَغ المُبَالَغَة** — the five patterns, why **فَعَّال** became the pattern of professions, and the names of Allah built on them

## 1. Āyah 6 — Translating It Together

## 2. يَجْتَبِيكَ — The Root of Being Chosen

### 2.1 اِجْتَبَى — To Choose, To Select

**اِجْتَبَى / يَجْتَبِي** is a **Form VIII** verb meaning *to choose, to select, to single out*.

| Arabic | Transliteration | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **اِجْتَبَى / يَجْتَبِي** | *ijtabā / yajtabī* | To choose, select (Form VIII) |
| **اِجْتِبَاء** | *ijtibāʾ* | Selection, choosing (maṣdar) |
| **مُجْتَبٍ** | *mujtabin* | The one who chooses (ism fāʿil) |
| **مُجْتَبًى** | *mujtaban* | The chosen one (ism mafʿūl) |

Its close relative is **اِصْطَفَى** — also Form VIII — from which comes **المُصْطَفَى**, *the Chosen One*, a title of the Prophet ﷺ.

!!! important "No Two Words Are Perfectly Equal"
    The teacher cautioned that saying **اِجْتَبَى** "has the same meaning as" **اِصْطَفَى** does not mean they are 100% identical. In Arabic — as in most languages — no two words are exact equivalents; there are always subtle differences of nuance. For dictionary purposes we may treat them as synonyms, but the difference is real.

### 2.2 In the Āyah

- **يَجْتَبِي** — the verb
- **رَبُّكَ** — the **فَاعِل** (the One who chooses)
- **كَ** — the **مَفْعُول** (the one chosen)

So Allah is **المُجْتَبِي** and Yūsuf عليه السلام is **المُجْتَبَى**.

## 3. اِسْتِخَارَة — Asking Allah to Choose

The root of choosing led the teacher into an extended, deliberately practical digression on **اِسْتِخَارَة**.

### 3.1 The Morphology

| Arabic | Analysis |
|---|---|
| **اِسْتِخَارَة** | Maṣdar, **Form X**, root **خ-ي-ر** |
| **اِسْتَخَارَ / يَسْتَخِير** | Form X verb |
| **خَيْر** | The Form I root meaning — *good, goodness* |

!!! note "Form X Carries the Meaning of *Seeking*"
    Compare **اِسْتَغْفَرَ** (Form X of **غَفَرَ**, *to forgive*) — which does not mean "to forgive" but **to seek forgiveness**. By exactly the same logic, **اِسْتِخَارَة** simply means **seeking khayr (goodness) from Allah**. Nothing more mysterious than that.

### 3.2 The Word Itself Dispels the Myths

!!! important "What Istikhāra Is Not"
    The teacher spoke strongly against the superstitions built around istikhāra — particularly widespread in the Indo-Pak subcontinent — that it is a kind of magical procedure with fixed protocols: that you must pray it immediately before sleeping, dress a certain way, and then *see* particular signs in a dream; and that if you saw the right signs, whatever you do afterwards *cannot* go wrong. The job you take cannot turn out toxic; the marriage you accept cannot fail.

    In her words, this is *"sheer nonsense."* Istikhāra is not that. It is simply asking your Lord for goodness, asking for His guidance, and asking Him to put **خَيْر** into whatever you are about to do.

### 3.3 Istikhāra Does Not Replace مَشْوَرَة

Consulting Allah does not remove the obligation to consult people and do your own work. **مَشْوَرَة** (mutual consultation) has enormous significance in the Quran and is not displaced by istikhāra.

!!! example "Due Diligence Alongside Duʿāʾ"
    Someone considering a marriage proposal still investigates the person, asks people who know them, and does their due diligence. Someone taking a new job still researches the role, the market, and asks specialists for their recommendations. Istikhāra runs *alongside* all of this — before it, during it, and after it — as we settle towards one option.

### 3.4 The X-Axis and the Y-Axis

!!! example "The Analogy the Teacher Uses with Her Children"
    Picture a point on a plain number line — the **x-axis** alone. The point may be moving forward, moving backward, or sitting perfectly still. If it is still, you conclude nothing is progressing.

    But that judgement only holds because you are looking at one axis. Add the **y-axis** — the second dimension — and the very same point that is stationary or even sliding backward on x may be climbing steeply on y.

    **Bringing Allah into our lives is adding that second dimension.** A decision made with istikhāra may still cost us materially: the business may lose money, the marriage may prove difficult. But what is lost on the x-axis is being gained on the y-axis — because we entered the endeavour asking Allah to place khayr in it, and He does.

### 3.5 The Short Duʿāʾ

Even when there is no time to pray the two rakʿahs, the istikhāra is not lost — there is a short duʿāʾ the Prophet ﷺ taught:

> **اللَّهُمَّ خِرْ لِي وَاخْتَرْ لِي**
>
> *"O Allah, choose good for me and choose for me."*

!!! tip "Make It a Habit for Small Decisions Too"
    The teacher urged students not to reserve istikhāra for life-altering decisions like marriage. Get into the habit of consulting Allah and asking for khayr before *anything* — small, simple, everyday decisions included.

## 4. الاِسْم المَنقُوص — Why مُجْتَبٍ Ends in Two Kasrahs

The ism fāʿil of **اِجْتَبَى** is **مُجْتَبٍ**, and the teacher drew attention to those two kasrahs at the end.

### 4.1 The Rule

!!! important "Definition"
    When the **last root letter** of a noun is a **وَاو** or a **يَاء**, the noun is an **اِسْم مَنقُوص**.

The underlying form is **مُجْتَبِيٌ** — with a **يَاء** carrying **تَنوِين الضَّمّ**. But a **يَاء** does not sit comfortably under a **ضَمَّة** with tanwīn. So:

1. The **يَاء** is dropped;
2. The **تَنوِين** that was on it transfers back to the letter before it, which carries a **كَسْرَة**;
3. The result is **مُجْتَبٍ** — a **double kasrah**.

!!! note "A Double Kasrah Does *Not* Mean Majrūr"
    The teacher was emphatic here: seeing **ـٍ** at the end of **مُجْتَبٍ** does **not** mean the word is **مَجرُور**. It is **مَنقُوص**. The ending is a *phonetic* consequence of the dropped yāʾ, not a case marker. (She also corrected her own slip mid-explanation: *manqūṣ*, not *maksūr*.)

!!! question "Can We Give the Iʿrāb of a Single Word?"
    A student's question prompted the reminder that iʿrāb only exists *within a structure* — a word standing alone has no iʿrāb, because iʿrāb reflects a word's **function** in a sentence.

### 4.2 The Three Times the يَاء Comes Back

The dropped **يَاء** returns in exactly three situations:

| # | Condition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The noun carries **ال** | **المُجْتَبِي** |
| 2 | The noun is a **مُضَاف** | **قَاضِي مَكَّة** *(the judge of Makkah)* |
| 3 | The noun is **مَنصُوب** | **رَأَيْتُ قَاضِيًا** |

!!! note "Why مَنصُوب Is Different"
    The **يَاء** has no phonetic trouble carrying a **فَتْحَة**. The problem was only ever with **ضَمَّة** and **كَسْرَة**. So in the manṣūb state the yāʾ simply stays.

## 5. الاِسْم المَقصُور — مُجْتَبًى and المُجْتَبَى

The **ism mafʿūl** is **مُجْتَبًى** — written with an **أَلِف**, not a **يَاء**.

!!! note "أَلِف مَقصُورَة, and Why It Is Written with an Alif"
    The final letter is an **أَلِف مَقصُورَة** (a "shortened" alif). The word is read **مُجْتَبَنْ** in continuous speech, but when you **stop** on it you read it with the alif — **مُجْتَبَا**. This is why it is *written* with an alif in the first place. The same spelling convention appears constantly in the Quran: words ending in **فَتْحَة + تَنوِين** are written with a final alif precisely to remind you how to pronounce them when you pause.

With **ال**, the tanwīn drops away and the word becomes definite — **المُجْتَبَى** — and here the **يَاء** stays omitted, because the yāʾ's difficulty is now with the **فَتْحَة**. The same holds for **المُصْطَفَى**.

!!! important "Phonetic, Not Grammatical"
    The teacher stressed that all of this — dropped yāʾs, transferred tanwīn, alif maqṣūrah spelling — belongs to **phonetics**, not to grammar. These are rules about how Arabic *sounds*, and the writing follows the sound.

## 6. تَأْوِيل — Interpretation as "Returning"

**تَأْوِيل** is the **maṣdar of Form II** from the root **أ-و-ل**.

| Form | Verb | Maṣdar | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | **آلَ / يَؤُولُ** | **أَوْل / مَآل** | To return (to something) |
| II | **أَوَّلَ / يُؤَوِّلُ** | **تَأْوِيل** | To return something → to explain / interpret it |

The Arabic definition the textbook gives is:

> **تَفْسِيرُ مَا يَؤُولُ إِلَيْهِ الشَّيْء**
>
> *"The explanation of that to which a thing returns."*

!!! example "Why 'Returning' Means 'Explaining'"
    Imagine you have a concept **A**, and you produce an explanation of it. What is that explanation doing? It is **bringing the meaning back to A**. An explanation is precisely something that returns you to the original thing's meaning — which is why the root of *returning* became the root of *interpreting*.

!!! note "Same Root, Familiar Word"
    **أَوَّل** — *first* — comes from this same root. What is first is what things return to.

!!! tip "On Machine Translation"
    The teacher openly checked Google Translate mid-session for **تَفْسِيرُ مَا يَؤُولُ إِلَيْهِ الشَّيْء** and got back *"interpreting what leads to creation"* — which she immediately rejected: there is no **خَلْق** in the phrase. The correct sense is *"interpretation of what it leads/returns to."* A useful live demonstration that machine translation of technical Arabic needs to be checked against your own grammar.

## 7. الأَحَادِيث — The Word حَدِيث

### 7.1 From Event, to New, to Speech

The teacher traced the semantic history of **حَدِيث**:

| Stage | Arabic | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| Root verb | **حَدَثَ** | Something *took place* — an event with an element of time in it |
| Adjectival | **حَدِيث** | *New* — e.g. **مُسْلِمٌ حَدِيثٌ**, "a new Muslim" |
| Extended | **حَدِيث** | An event that took place → the *account* of it |
| Final | **حَدِيث** | Any speech, any talk at all |

!!! note "The Order of Development"
    The meaning did not begin with "speech." It began with **event** — something that happens in time — and only afterwards came to mean the *report* of an event, and finally speech in general.

### 7.2 A سَمَاعِي Plural

The plural of **حَدِيث** is **أَحَادِيث**.

!!! important "No Rule Governs This Plural"
    Normally a given noun pattern takes a predictable broken plural. **أَحَادِيث** is **سَمَاعِي** — *heard*, i.e. learned from usage rather than derived by rule. There is no pattern that dictates why **حَدِيث** should pluralise this way. It is simply what the Arabs said, and we take it as it is.

### 7.3 The Arabic Definition

> **مَا يُتَحَدَّثُ بِهِ وَيُنْقَل**
>
> *"What is spoken about and transmitted."*

This is exactly why the term covers so much in Islamic sciences: the **speech** of the Prophet ﷺ, his **actions**, the occasions on which he **remained silent**, and the things he **prohibited** — all of it is **حَدِيث**, because all of it is what was talked about and passed on.

### 7.4 The Quran Calls Itself حَدِيث

!!! example "سُورَة المُرْسَلَات — the final āyah"
    > **فَبِأَيِّ حَدِيثٍ بَعْدَهُ يُؤْمِنُونَ**
    >
    > *"Then in what ḥadīth after this will they believe?"*

    Here **حَدِيث** is the Quran itself: *after this speech, what other speech is going to make them believe?*

!!! example "سُورَة الزُّمَر"
    > **اللَّهُ نَزَّلَ أَحْسَنَ الْحَدِيثِ**
    >
    > *"Allah has revealed the best of speech."*

## 8. تَأْوِيل الأَحَادِيث — Six Opinions

The mufassirūn have explained **تَأْوِيل الأَحَادِيث** in a range of ways:

| # | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Interpretation of dreams** — Yūsuf will be given the very knowledge he has just experienced |
| 2 | **The knowledge of explaining knowledge** — the ability to take difficult matters and make them clear |
| 3 | **الحِكْمَة** — he will be given wisdom, and be able to explain wisdom to people |
| 4 | **Narrations of previous generations** and the previous books |
| 5 | **The proofs of tawḥīd** — special knowledge enabling him to expound them |
| 6 | **The previous scriptures** and the sunan of those before |

!!! important "The Majority View"
    Across the tafāsīr the teacher has read, most scholars go with the first reading, combined with the phrase's larger sense: **Allah will choose you for prophethood, and will teach you from this special knowledge — the interpretation of dreams.**

!!! info "Confirmed by the Narrative Itself"
    This reading is borne out later in the sūrah: Yūsuf عليه السلام interprets the dreams of his two fellow prisoners and then the dream of the king. Just as every prophet was given some particular gift or miracle as his speciality, **this was the speciality of Yūsuf عليه السلام**.

## 9. يُعَلِّمُ and يُتِمُّ — Reading Forms Off the Root

### 9.1 عَلِمَ → عَلَّمَ → تَعَلَّمَ

| Form | Verb | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| I | **عَلِمَ** | To know |
| II | **عَلَّمَ** | To teach |
| V | **تَعَلَّمَ** | To learn |

!!! note "How Arabic Defines 'To Teach'"
    The textbook's Arabic gloss for **عَلَّمَ** is **جَعَلَهُ يَتَعَلَّمُهُ** — *to make him learn it*. Teaching is defined as *causing learning*, which is exactly the relationship between Form II (causative) and Form V (reflexive/acquisitive) of the same root.

### 9.2 تَمَّ → أَتَمَّ

| Form | Verb | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| I | **تَمَّ** | **To be** complete (in itself) |
| IV | **أَتَمَّ** | **To complete** something else |

!!! note "A Correction Made Live"
    The teacher first glossed **تَمَّ** as "to complete" and immediately corrected herself: **تَمَّ** means *to become complete* — the completion happens *to* the subject. It is **أَتَمَّ**, Form IV, that means *someone else completed it*. That is exactly the form used in the āyah — **يُتِمُّ نِعْمَتَهُ**, *He completes His favour*, and **كَمَا أَتَمَّهَا**, *just as He completed it*.

The Arabic gloss offered was **كَمُلَ** for **تَمَّ** — *to be whole*.

## 10. نِعْمَة — Defined in Arabic

!!! info "A Shift in Method — Arabic-Only Explanation"
    From this section onward the teacher noted something about the textbook itself: it now explains vocabulary **in Arabic**, without giving meanings in any other language. Nothing in the grammar of these āyāt is new — she checked her notes and found no concept that had not already been covered in earlier sessions. The point of this section is therefore **practice in reading grammatical explanation in Arabic**, a deliberate transition towards Arabic-only dictionaries.

Where previously we would simply answer *"blessings"* or *"favours"* for **نِعْمَة**, the Arabic definition is:

> **مَا أُنْعِمَ بِهِ عَلَى الإِنْسَانِ مِنَ الرِّزْقِ وَغَيْرِهِ**
>
> *"That which a person has been favoured with, of sustenance and other than it."*

| Arabic | Note |
|---|---|
| **نِعْمَة** | Singular |
| **نِعَم** | Plural |
| **أَنْعُم** | Also a plural |
| **أَنْعَمَ عَلَيْهِ** | *He bestowed upon him / favoured him* — the verbal usage |

## 11. Stacking Attached Pronouns — and When إِيَّا Is Needed

Verbs that take **two objects** can have both of them as pronouns. The teacher set out when the two may be joined onto the verb and when they may not.

### 11.1 Different Persons — Stacking Is Allowed

If the two attached pronouns belong to **different persons**, they may sit one after the other on the verb:

> **أَعْطَيْتُكَهُ** — *I gave it to you*
>
> **كَ** = 2nd person · **هُ** = 3rd person → different persons, so they stack.

### 11.2 Same Person — Stacking Is Forbidden

If both pronouns are from the **same** person — two 3rd-person pronouns, or two 2nd-person pronouns — they **cannot** be stacked. In that case the **detached (مُنفَصِل)** pronoun must be brought in.

The problem is that **هُ** in its manṣūb and majrūr forms is **مُتَّصِل** — an attached pronoun that *must* cling to something. To have it standing free, you need **إِيَّاهُ**.

!!! example "إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ"
    In **سُورَة الفَاتِحَة**, **كَ** is likewise an attached pronoun. Had the order been **نَعْبُدُكَ**, the **كَ** would simply attach to the verb and all would be well. But Allah brings the object **before** the verb — and an attached pronoun cannot stand alone in that position. Hence **إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ**.

!!! question "Is إِيَّا Part of the Pronoun, or a Separate Particle?"
    Scholars differ. Some hold that **إِيَّاكَ** as a whole is one word — the detached manṣūb form of the pronoun. Others hold that **إِيَّا** is a distinct particle attached to the pronoun in order to detach it. Either way, the teacher noted, **it serves the same purpose**.

## 12. آل and أَبَوَيْكَ — The Family of Yaʿqūb

### 12.1 آل

**آل** covers a range of related senses:

| Sense | Note |
|---|---|
| The people close to a person | The core meaning |
| The immediate family | Its use in this āyah |
| Followers / adherents | E.g. of a leader or teacher |
| A community | The widest sense |

!!! note "In آلِ يَعْقُوبَ"
    Here **آل** *does* mean the immediate family of Yaʿqūb عليه السلام — but it also extends downward to the descendants further along the line: grandchildren, great-grandchildren, and so on.

### 12.2 أَبَوَيْكَ — The Dual

**أَبَوَيْكَ** is the **dual** of **أَب**.

- The independent dual is **أَبَوَانِ**;
- In the manṣūb/majrūr state it is **أَبَوَيْنِ**;
- Because it becomes a **مُضَاف** (to **كَ**), the **نُون** is dropped → **أَبَوَيْ + كَ = أَبَوَيْكَ**.

!!! important "Who Are the 'Two Fathers'?"
    Not father and grandfather — the āyah names them: **إِبْرَاهِيم** and **إِسْحَاق**. So relative to Yūsuf عليه السلام, these are his **grandfather** (Isḥāq) and his **great-grandfather** (Ibrāhīm). Arabic freely uses **أَب** for ancestors up the line.

## 13. حَكِيم — صِفَة مُشَبَّهَة

**حَكِيم** is glossed in Arabic as **صَاحِبُ الحِكْمَة** — *the possessor of wisdom*.

!!! note "Not an Ism Fāʿil"
    The teacher asked what kind of word **حَكِيم** is and confirmed it is **not** an **اِسْم فَاعِل** — it is a **صِفَة مُشَبَّهَة** (see [[sifat-mushabbaha]]), the pattern that denotes a **settled, permanent** quality rather than an act being performed.

Note also that **صَاحِبُ الحِكْمَة** is not a **جُمْلَة** — it is a **مُضَاف + مُضَاف إِلَيْه** construction, a phrase, not a sentence.

## 14. كَذَٰلِكَ — A نَائِب عَن المَفْعُول المُطْلَق

### 14.1 The Iʿrāb of كَذَٰلِكَ

**كَذَٰلِكَ** breaks into:

- **كَ** — a **حَرْف جَرّ**
- **ذَٰلِكَ** — **مَجرُور** by it

!!! question "What Is the عَلَامَة of ذَٰلِكَ Being Majrūr?"
    A student offered the **كَسْرَة**. The teacher pushed back: **ذَٰلِكَ** is a demonstrative — it is **مَبْنِي**, it never changes its ending. So we do not say it is *majrūr with a kasrah*; we say it is:

    > **مَجْرُور مَحَلًّا** — *majrūr in position*.

    For **مَبْنِي** words we always speak of **مَحَلًّا** iʿrāb, because no visible sign can appear on them. (Compare: for a normal noun like **كِتَاب** we would say **مَجْرُور وَعَلَامَةُ جَرِّهِ الكَسْرَة**.)

### 14.2 The Whole Phrase Is مَنصُوب

Here is the subtlety that takes getting used to: although **كَذَٰلِكَ** is internally a **جَارّ وَمَجرُور**, the phrase as a whole occupies the position of a **مَفْعُول مُطْلَق** — it is **مَنصُوب عَلَى المَفْعُولِيَّة المُطْلَقَة**.

!!! example "The Replacement Test"
    Substitute the phrase and the structure becomes visible:

    > **يَجْتَبِيكَ رَبُّكَ اِجْتِبَاءً مِثْلَ ذَٰلِكَ الاِجْتِبَاء**
    >
    > *"Your Lord will choose you with a choosing like that choosing."*

    **كَذَٰلِكَ** stands in for **اِجْتِبَاءً مِثْلَ ذَٰلِكَ** — which is why it is a **نَائِب عَن المَفْعُول المُطْلَق**: the maṣdar itself is dropped and its describer takes its place. (See [[mafool-mutlaq]] for the wider family of deputising constructions.)

### 14.3 What Is ذَٰلِكَ Pointing To?

It points back to **the selection Yūsuf has just experienced** — the dream itself.

!!! info "Yaʿqūb's Meaning"
    Just as Allah chose you for **these majestic dreams** — this dream in which the stars, the sun and the moon prostrated to you, a dream that itself points toward a **lofty station** — so too will your Lord choose you **for great matters**: for prophethood and what comes with it. The dream is the sign; the selection for nubuwwah is what it signals.

    Find further examples in the Quran of **كَذَٰلِكَ** functioning as a **مَفْعُول مُطْلَق**, and we will dissect each one the way this one was dissected.

## 15. The Anatomy of ذَٰلِكَ

**ذَٰلِكَ** is built from three pieces:

| Piece | Name | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **ذَا** | The demonstrative proper | *This* |
| **لَ** | **لَام البُعْد** | Marks distance |
| **كَ** | **كَاف الخِطَاب** | Addresses the listener |

### 15.1 Dropping the لَام

Remove the **لَام** and you get **ذَاكَ** — used for something **less far away**.

!!! example "Near, Far, Farther"
    A star is **ذَٰلِكَ**. A tree you can see down the road is **ذَاكَ**.

### 15.2 The كَاف Changes with the Addressee

The **كَاف الخِطَاب** agrees with **who you are speaking to** — not with what you are pointing at:

| Form | Addressee |
|---|---|
| **ذَٰلِكَ** | One male |
| **ذَٰلِكُمْ** | A group |
| **ذَٰلِكُنَّ** | A group of females (less common) |

!!! example "ذَٰلِكُمْ أَزْكَىٰ لَكُمْ — سُورَة البَقَرَة"
    > **ذَٰلِكُمْ أَزْكَىٰ لَكُمْ وَأَطْهَرُ ۗ وَاللَّهُ يَعْلَمُ وَأَنتُمْ لَا تَعْلَمُونَ**
    >
    > *"That is purer for you and cleaner; and Allah knows while you do not know."*

    The context: divorced women who have completed their ʿiddah and wish to remarry their former husbands — where the divorce was not the third, and both husband and wife recognise it was a mistake. Allah instructs the guardians not to prevent them.

!!! important "Plural كَاف Does Not Mean Plural Referent"
    Only **one thing** is being pointed at here. **ذَٰلِكَ** becoming **ذَٰلِكُمْ** does **not** mean the thing pointed to has become plural — it means **the people being addressed** are now more than one. If we were pointing to more than one thing we would use **أُولَٰئِكَ**.

## 16. مِن التَّبعِيضِيَّة — The Partitive مِن

In **وَيُعَلِّمُكَ مِن تَأْوِيلِ الْأَحَادِيثِ**, the **مِن** is **تَبعِيضِيَّة** — it means **بَعْض**, *some of*, not the whole.

| Without مِن | With مِن |
|---|---|
| **قَرَأْتُ الكِتَابَ** — *I read the book* (all of it) | **قَرَأْتُ مِنَ الكِتَابِ** — *I read from the book* (part of it) |
| **كُلِ الطَّعَامَ** — *eat the food* (all of it) | **كُلْ مِنْهُ** — *eat from it* (some of it) |

!!! example "وَمِمَّا رَزَقْنَاهُمْ يُنفِقُونَ"
    > **وَمِمَّا رَزَقْنَاهُمْ يُنفِقُونَ**
    >
    > *"…and from what We have provided them, they spend."*

    Among the qualities of the believers: they believe in the unseen, establish the prayer, and spend **from** what Allah has given them — **some of it**, not all of it.

!!! tip "Look at the Choice of Words"
    The teacher paused on the sheer courtesy of this construction. Everything we possess was given to us by Allah in the first place — yet even so He does not ask us to spend all of it.

    *"If I give you $100 and then tell you to give that $100 to so-and-so, I am completely within my rights, because I gave you the $100. But no — Allah gives, and then asks only for* **some** *of it."*

## 17. كَمَا أَتَمَّهَا — مَا المَصْدَرِيَّة

In **كَمَا أَتَمَّهَا**, the **مَا** is **مَصْدَرِيَّة** — it converts the following verb into a maṣdar:

> **كَمَا أَتَمَّهَا** ≡ **كَإِتْمَامِهَا** — *"like His completing it."*

This is the same device covered in [Session 5](session-5.md), and it works identically here.

## 18. قَبْلُ and بَعْدُ — The Full Rule

The āyah reads **مِن قَبْلُ** — with a **ضَمَّة**, not the **كَسْرَة** you would expect after **مِن**. The teacher laid out the complete three-case rule.

### 18.1 Case 1 — Muḍāf ilayhi Omitted **but Intended** → مَبْنِي عَلَى الضَّمّ

!!! important "Two Conditions Together"
    **قَبْلُ** and **بَعْدُ** are built on the **ضَمَّة** when:

    1. The **مُضَاف إِلَيْه** is **omitted** (**حُذِفَ المُضَافُ إِلَيْه**); **and**
    2. It is nevertheless **intended** (**نُوِيَ مَعْنَاهُ**) — i.e. you *do* mean "before *that particular thing*," you simply do not say it.

!!! example "A Simple Illustration"
    > **أَنَا الآنَ مُدَرِّسٌ، وَكُنْتُ مِنْ قَبْلُ إِدَارِيًّا**
    >
    > *"I am now a teacher; before that I was an administrator."*

    "Before *that*" — before being a teacher — is fully intended, just not spelled out. Hence **قَبْلُ**.

**In our āyah:** *"He completed His favour upon you — and before this, He completed the same favour upon your fathers Ibrāhīm and Isḥāq."* The "before *this*" is clearly meant; it is simply not stated. Hence **مِن قَبْلُ**.

#### Quranic Evidence

!!! example "سُورَة الرُّوم — لِلَّهِ الْأَمْرُ مِن قَبْلُ وَمِن بَعْدُ"
    > **لِلَّهِ الْأَمْرُ مِن قَبْلُ وَمِن بَعْدُ ۚ وَيَوْمَئِذٍ يَفْرَحُ الْمُؤْمِنُونَ**

    The context is the Romans: they have been defeated, but within a few years they will overpower the Persians. Both the defeat **and** the eventual victory belong to Allah's command — **before** their defeat and **after** it. Two instances of the built form in one āyah.

!!! example "سُورَة يُونُس — آلْآنَ وَقَدْ عَصَيْتَ قَبْلُ"
    > **آلْآنَ وَقَدْ عَصَيْتَ قَبْلُ وَكُنتَ مِنَ الْمُفْسِدِينَ**

    Spoken to Firʿawn as he drowns and declares belief in the Lord of Mūsā. **قَبْلُ** here unmistakably means **before this moment** — before the moment in which you are now proclaiming īmān. The muḍāf ilayhi is omitted but absolutely intended.

!!! example "سُورَة القَصَص — أَوَلَمْ يَكْفُرُوا بِمَا أُوتِيَ مُوسَىٰ مِن قَبْلُ"
    > **أَوَلَمْ يَكْفُرُوا بِمَا أُوتِيَ مُوسَىٰ مِن قَبْلُ ۖ قَالُوا سِحْرَانِ تَظَاهَرَا وَقَالُوا إِنَّا بِكُلٍّ كَافِرُونَ**

    When the truth came to them from Allah they said, *"If only he were given the like of what Mūsā was given"* — meaning the Tawrāh, or the miracles of Mūsā عليه السلام. Allah answers: *did you not already reject what Mūsā was given, before this?* **مِن قَبْلُ** may here mean before the coming of the Quran, or before the coming of the Messenger ﷺ — **both meanings are available.**

    And their own words condemn them: **سِحْرَانِ تَظَاهَرَا** — *"two pieces of magic supporting each other"* — since the Tawrāh attests the Quran and the Quran attests the Tawrāh; **إِنَّا بِكُلٍّ كَافِرُونَ** — *"we disbelieve in all of them."*

!!! note "A Grammar Aside — the Dual Alif"
    Working through the dual verb here, the teacher pointed out the **أَلِف** of the dual. In an ordinary active verb (**ضَرَبَا**) that alif is the **فَاعِل**. In the **مَجهُول** the same alif is instead the **نَائِب الفَاعِل** — the deputy subject. The position it occupies is the same; only its name changes with the voice of the verb.

### 18.2 Case 2 — Muḍāf ilayhi **Mentioned** → مُعْرَب

When the **مُضَاف إِلَيْه** is actually present, **قَبْل** and **بَعْد** decline completely normally:

> **مِن قَبْلِهِ** — the **هُ** is the muḍāf ilayhi, so **قَبْلِ** is simply **مَجرُور** with a kasrah. Not **مَبْنِي** at all.

### 18.3 Case 3 — Muḍāf ilayhi Neither Mentioned **nor Intended** → مُعْرَب, and Given تَنوِين

The third situation: the muḍāf ilayhi is omitted **and you do not even intend one** — you are not thinking "before *what*" at all. Then **قَبْل** and **بَعْد** are **مُعْرَب** and take **تَنوِين**:

> **قَبْلًا** — *previously* · **بَعْدًا** — *later*

!!! note "لَمْ يُنْوَ"
    The Arabic phrasing of the condition is **لَمْ يُنْوَ** — *"it was not intended."* The teacher walked through the morphology: from **نِيَّة** → **نَوَى / يَنْوِي** → passive **يُنْوَى** → jussive **لَمْ يُنْوَ**, where the final weak letter drops because of the **جَزْم** caused by **لَمْ**.

!!! example "Everyday Illustrations"
    > **كُنْتُ مُدَرِّسًا قَبْلًا** — *"I was a teacher previously"* — with no particular "before what" in mind.
    >
    > **سَأَزُورُكَ بَعْدًا إِنْ شَاءَ اللَّه** — *"I will visit you later"* — not after ṣalāh, not after dinner; just **later**.

!!! note "Iʿrāb of بَعْدًا"
    A student correctly identified it as **مَنصُوب**, and the teacher confirmed the reason: it is a **ظَرْف زَمَان** — an adverb of time. Even though "later" names no precise moment, it is still a unit of time.

!!! tip "English Equivalents"
    The teacher suggested that the best English renderings of this third case are exactly **"previously"** and **"later"** — words that carry no implied complement.

#### Poetic Evidence

!!! example "فَسَاغَ لِيَ الشَّرَابُ وَكُنْتُ قَبْلًا"
    > **فَسَاغَ لِيَ الشَّرَابُ وَكُنْتُ قَبْلًا** ⁂ **أَكَادُ أَغَصُّ بِالْمَاءِ الفُرَاتِ**
    >
    > *"So the drink went down easily for me — and previously I would almost choke on sweet water."*

    **سَاغَ** means *to become easy to swallow*, and its **فَاعِل** is **الشَّرَاب**. The poet's point: he was in such a state of distress before that even sweet water made him splutter and choke — whereas hot water or hot tea goes down easily on the throat — and now that his condition has changed, even the wine goes down easily. And **قَبْلًا** here is simply "previously": before *what* is neither specified nor intended.

!!! example "وَنَحْنُ قَتَلْنَا الأُسْدَ أُسْدَ خَفِيَّةٍ"
    > **وَنَحْنُ قَتَلْنَا الأُسْدَ أُسْدَ خَفِيَّةٍ** ⁂ **فَمَا شَرِبُوا بَعْدًا عَلَى لَذَّةٍ خَمْرَا**
    >
    > *"And we — we killed the lions, the lions of Khafiyyah; so never after did they drink wine for pleasure."*

    **الأُسْد** is a plural of **أَسَد** (lion). **خَفِيَّة** was a place of dense, thorny thicket where lions dwelt. The poet is boasting about killing enemy warriors — "the lions of Khafiyyah."

    Note the emphasis in **وَنَحْنُ قَتَلْنَا**: **قَتَلْنَا** alone would have sufficed, since the **نَا** already indicates the subject. Beginning with the detached **نَحْنُ** is emphatic — *it is* **we** *who killed them*.

    And **بَعْدًا** does **not** mean "after killing them." It means simply **thereafter, ever again** — they were never able to enjoy wine again. The muḍāf ilayhi is not intended, and the **تَنوِين** is precisely what tells us so. (Wine, as the teacher noted, was a large part of their life and keeps surfacing in their poetry.)

## 19. جَمْع مُنْتَهَى الجُمُوع and مَمْنُوع مِن الصَّرْف

### 19.1 The Plural of Plurals

Arabic can form a **plural of a plural**. Taking **بَيْت**:

| Word | Note |
|---|---|
| **بَيْت** | A house — **and also a couplet of poetry** |
| **بُيُوت** | Plural |
| **أَبْيَات** | Also a plural |

!!! question "Homework 3 — An Open Question"
    The teacher was candid about her uncertainty: is **أَبْيَات** the plural of **بَيْت** in the sense of *couplets*, while **بُيُوت** is the plural in the sense of *houses*? Or is **أَبْيَات** a **جَمْع الجَمْع** — a plural of **بُيُوت**? She undertook to check and report back.

!!! important "Definition — جَمْع مُنْتَهَى الجُمُوع"
    Whatever the answer, the general principle stands: some words admit a further plural of their plural — but there comes a **last** plural beyond which **no further plural can be made**. That final plural is called **جَمْع مُنْتَهَى الجُمُوع**, *the plural of the utmost plurals*.

!!! question "Student Question — Must It Be a Plural of a Plural?"
    A student asked whether **جَمْع مُنْتَهَى الجُمُوع** necessarily means a plural formed *from* another plural, or simply the limit of pluralisation.

    **Answer:** it is the **limit**. It need not be built on another plural at all. **مَسَاجِد** is a case in point — it is a plural of **مَسْجِد**, not of any other plural — yet you cannot form a further plural from **مَسَاجِد**. It is at the limit, and that is what qualifies it.

### 19.2 The Patterns مَفَاعِل and مَفَاعِيل

You recognise a **جَمْع مُنْتَهَى الجُمُوع** by its **pattern**: anything on the shape of **مَفَاعِل** or **مَفَاعِيل** (or resembling them).

!!! example "Ibn Mālik's Alfiyyah — the Rule of Prevention"
    > **وَكُنْ لِجَمْعٍ مُشْبِهٍ مَفَاعِلَا** ⁂ **أَوِ المَفَاعِيلَ بِمَنْعٍ كَافِلَا**
    >
    > *"And be, for a plural resembling* **مَفَاعِل** *or* **المَفَاعِيل**, *a guarantor of prevention."*

    **كَافِل** — guarantor, custodian, one who takes charge. The line instructs the student: *be the enforcer of* **مَنْع** *(prevention from tanwīn)* for any plural on these patterns. In other words, such plurals are **مَمْنُوع مِن الصَّرْف** — diptote.

!!! note "Poetic Licence"
    In **مَفَاعِلَا**, the extra **أَلِف** is not part of the word — it is added for the metre of the poem. The actual pattern is **مَفَاعِل**.

### 19.3 When a Diptote Takes a كَسْرَة Anyway

A **مَمْنُوع مِن الصَّرْف** noun is normally **majrūr with a fatḥah**. But there are **two conditions** under which it reverts to taking a **كَسْرَة** — becoming, in effect, triptote again:

| # | Condition |
|---|---|
| 1 | It is a **مُضَاف** |
| 2 | It carries **ال** |

!!! example "Ibn Mālik's Alfiyyah — the Exception"
    > **وَجُرَّ بِالفَتْحَةِ مَا لَا يَنْصَرِفْ** ⁂ **مَا لَمْ يُضَفْ أَوْ يَكُ بَعْدَ أَلْ رَدِفْ**
    >
    > *"And put into jarr with the fatḥah that which does not fully decline — so long as it is not made a muḍāf, or does not follow behind* **ال**."

**Working through the couplet's own grammar:**

- **جُرَّ** is **مَجهُول** (built with a fatḥah). Its **نَائِب الفَاعِل** is **مَا**.
- That **مَا** is **مَوْصُولَة** — *that which*.
- **مَا لَا يَنْصَرِف** — *that which does not fully decline* (i.e. does not conjugate through the full set of endings with tanwīn).
- **رَدِفْ** is a verb whose **فَاعِل** is the diptote noun: it "rides behind" **ال**.

!!! note "Poetic Licence Again"
    Here too the teacher pointed out an added **أَلِف** — **أَلْ** written out with an alif to serve the metre. The word itself is just **ال**.

!!! info "رَدِفَ — To Ride Behind"
    **رَدِفَ** means to ride behind someone — either on the **same** mount, sharing the saddle, or on the animal immediately following. A **رَدِيف** is that second rider.

    The word appears often in ḥadīth. In one narration **مُعَاذ بن جَبَل** رضي الله عنه relates that he was **رِدْف النَّبِيِّ ﷺ** — the Prophet's ﷺ companion-rider, seated in the same saddle behind him — before the Prophet ﷺ gave him the well-known naṣīḥah that follows. It appears likewise in the narration of **الفَضْل بن عَبَّاس** during Ḥajj. Searching the word **رَدِيف** in the ḥadīth collections turns up a whole family of such reports.

!!! example "Examples of the Two Conditions"
    **مَدَارِس** is on the pattern **مَفَاعِل** and is therefore diptote — but as a **مُضَاف** it takes a **كَسْرَة**.

    > **فَلَا أُقْسِمُ بِمَوَاقِعِ النُّجُومِ**
    >
    > *"So I swear by the setting places of the stars."*

    **مَوَاقِع** is likewise on the pattern **مَفَاعِل** and diptote — but here it is the **مُضَاف** of **النُّجُوم**, so it too takes a **كَسْرَة** rather than a fatḥah.

### 19.4 إِبْرَاهِيم — A بَدَل That Is Also a Diptote

In **عَلَىٰ أَبَوَيْكَ مِن قَبْلُ إِبْرَاهِيمَ وَإِسْحَاقَ**:

- **إِبْرَاهِيم** is a **بَدَل** from **أَبَوَيْكَ** — it names the two fathers explicitly.
- A **بَدَل** follows the iʿrāb of its **مُبْدَل مِنْه**. **أَبَوَيْ** comes after **عَلَىٰ**, so it is **مَجرُور** — therefore **إِبْرَاهِيم** is **مَجرُور** too.
- But it appears with a **فَتْحَة**, because it is **مَمْنُوع مِن الصَّرْف**.
- And *why* is it diptote? Because it is a **non-Arab (عَجَمِي) proper name**.

## 20. صِيَغ المُبَالَغَة — The Patterns of Intensity

The āyah closes with **عَلِيمٌ حَكِيمٌ**, which brought the class back to **صِيَغ المُبَالَغَة** — patterns of hyperbole, covered in detail in an earlier session and revisited here. There are **five** such patterns.

!!! important "What مُبَالَغَة Means"
    Not simply *one who does X* (that is the **اِسْم فَاعِل**), but **one who does X a great deal, over and over again**.

    From **غَفَرَ** (*to forgive*): **غَافِر** is the ism fāʿil — *one who forgives*. **غَفَّار** is the intensive — *one who forgives again and again and again*.

### 20.1 The Patterns Discussed

| Pattern | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **فَعَّال** | **غَفَّار**, **وَهَّاب**, **خَلَّاق** | Very common; **also the pattern of professions** |
| **فَعُول** | **شَكُور**, **غَفُور**, **وَدُود** | Very common |
| **فَعِيل** | **عَلِيم**, **حَكِيم** | Very common |

!!! note "Three Common, Two Rare"
    Of the five patterns, three are found very commonly — in the Quran generally and in the Names of Allah in particular. The remaining two are much less common; the teacher could not name a Divine Name on one of them and invited the class to look and share anything they find. She also noted that one of the rarer forms is a **noun**, not an **اِسْم فَاعِل**.

### 20.2 فَعَّال and the Professions

!!! example "Why Professions Take فَعَّال"
    **حَلَقَ** means *to cut hair*; **حَالِق** is the ordinary ism fāʿil — *one cutting hair*. But **حَلَّاق** is **a barber** — the profession.

    Likewise **خَبَّاز** — *a baker*.

    The teacher noted that this is really still hyperbole: **a barber cuts hair all day long**. The professional *is* the person who does the action over and over. Other patterns are not used for professions in the same way — but that does not stop **فَعَّال** from being used for ordinary hyperbole too.

### 20.3 Names of Allah on These Patterns

!!! important "Names Exclusive to Allah"
    **غَفَّار** — the One who forgives again and again — is a Name of Allah, and **we cannot give this name to anyone else**. The same applies to **شَكُور**.

!!! info "شُكْر from Allah Is Not Gratitude"
    When **شُكْر** is attributed to a servant it means **being grateful** to Allah. When **شَكُور** is attributed to Allah it does **not** mean gratitude — it means **being gracious**: that He accepts our deeds and gives us **جَزَاء** for them.

Other Names on these patterns discussed: **وَهَّاب** — the One who keeps giving; **خَلَّاق** — the One who creates abundantly.

!!! question "Student Correction — وَهَّاب vs. وَدُود"
    A student queried the meaning of **وَهَّاب**, and the teacher initially glossed it as "loving." The student corrected her: **وَهَّاب** is *the Giver* — from **وَهَبَ**, *to bestow, to give as a gift*. The teacher accepted the correction gladly, noting she had confused it with **وَدُود**.

    **وَدُود** — on the pattern **فَعُول** — is *the Most Loving*, from **وُدّ** (love).

    > **إِنَّ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ سَيَجْعَلُ لَهُمُ الرَّحْمَٰنُ وُدًّا**
    >
    > *"Indeed those who believe and do righteous deeds — the Most Merciful will appoint for them love."*

## 23. Key Lessons from This Session

!!! tip "Summary of Lessons"
    1. **Form X means seeking.** Just as **اِسْتَغْفَرَ** is *to seek forgiveness*, **اِسْتِخَارَة** is simply *to seek khayr* — and the morphology alone is enough to dismantle the elaborate superstitions built around the practice.
    2. Istikhāra does not replace **مَشْوَرَة**, research, or due diligence — it accompanies them. And a decision made with istikhāra can still fail materially; what is lost on one axis is being gained on another.
    3. A **double kasrah** at the end of a word is not proof of **jarr**. In an **اِسْم مَنقُوص** it is a phonetic residue of a dropped **يَاء**, which returns only when the word has **ال**, is a **مُضَاف**, or is **مَنصُوب**.
    4. Much of what looks like grammar in the endings of **مُجْتَبٍ** and **المُجْتَبَى** is in fact **phonetics** — Arabic writing follows Arabic sound.
    5. **تَأْوِيل** is built on *returning*, because an explanation is precisely what returns you to a thing's meaning. Etymology, once again, carries the concept.
    6. **حَدِيث** began as *event*, passed through *new*, and only then became *speech* — which is why the Prophet's ﷺ silences and prohibitions are as much ḥadīth as his words, and why the Quran calls itself **حَدِيث**.
    7. Some plurals are **سَمَاعِي** — heard, not derived. **أَحَادِيث** has no rule behind it; it is simply what the Arabs said.
    8. For **مَبْنِي** words, iʿrāb is always **مَحَلًّا** — "in position." A demonstrative like **ذَٰلِكَ** can be *majrūr in position* while showing no sign of it at all.
    9. **كَذَٰلِكَ** is internally a **جَارّ وَمَجرُور** yet functions as a **نَائِب عَن المَفْعُول المُطْلَق** — a reminder that a phrase's internal structure and its role in the sentence are two different questions.
    10. In **ذَٰلِكُمْ**, the plural belongs to the **addressee**, not to the thing pointed at. Pointing to several things requires **أُولَٰئِكَ**.
    11. **مِن التَّبعِيضِيَّة** carries theological weight: everything we own was given by Allah, yet He asks us to spend only **some** of it.
    12. **قَبْلُ / بَعْدُ** have a three-way rule turning entirely on whether the muḍāf ilayhi is *mentioned*, *omitted-but-intended*, or *not intended at all* — and the ending (**ـُ**, **ـِ**, **ـًا**) tells you which, every time.
    13. A **جَمْع مُنْتَهَى الجُمُوع** is defined by being the **limit** of pluralisation, not by being built on another plural — **مَسَاجِد** proves the point.
    14. A diptote reverts to the **كَسْرَة** in exactly two situations: when it is a **مُضَاف**, and when it carries **ال** — the whole content of one couplet of the Alfiyyah.
    15. Poetry cited as **شَاهِد** must be read with an eye for **poetic licence**: both Alfiyyah couplets here carry an added alif purely for the metre.
    16. **صِيَغ المُبَالَغَة** are about *repetition of the act*, which is why **فَعَّال** naturally became the pattern of professions — a barber is someone who cuts hair all day long.
    17. **شُكْر** reverses direction depending on who is described: from the servant it is gratitude; from Allah, **شَكُور** means graciousness — acceptance and reward.
    18. Corrections in class run both ways: the teacher accepted a student's correction on **وَهَّاب** without hesitation. Engagement — unmuting, asking, correcting — is what lets a teacher calibrate the pace.
