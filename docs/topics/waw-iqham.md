# وَاو الاِقتِحَام — The Intrusive Wāw

**وَاو الاِقتِحَام** is the name the **Kūfī** grammarians give to a wāw they regard as **extra**: it is not a wāw of ʿaṭf, and the sentence it stands on is read as though it were not there. It lets them find the **jawāb** of **لَمَّا** or **حَتَّىٰ إِذَا** in the text, where the **Baṣrīs** say the jawāb is omitted.

---

## The Name

**اِقتِحَام** is the Form VIII maṣdar of ق-ح-م: *to push or drag in, to introduce forcibly, to cram; to plunge or rush; to burst*. The wāw has, so to speak, been pushed into the sentence.

## The Two Schools

| | Baṣrī | Kūfī |
|---|---|---|
| **Jawāb** | **مَحذُوف**, understood from context | In the text, on the verb after the wāw |
| **The wāw** | Keeps its normal role (ʿaṭf, or **ḥāl**) | Extra: **وَاو الاِقتِحَام** |

## Examples from the Quran

!!! example "Yūsuf 15"
    > **فَلَمَّا ذَهَبُوا بِهِ وَأَجْمَعُوا أَن يَجْعَلُوهُ فِي غَيَابَتِ الْجُبِّ ۚ وَأَوْحَيْنَا إِلَيْهِ**

    - **Baṣrī:** the jawāb is omitted: *they did so*, that is, they threw him in. The āyah never actually mentions it.
    - **Kūfī:** the jawāb is on one of the two wāw-verbs. **al-Ṭabarī** takes **وَأَجْمَعُوا** (at that moment they finally agreed); **al-Qurṭubī** takes **وَأَوْحَيْنَا** (at that moment Allah sent the waḥy).

!!! example "al-Ṣāffāt 103–104"
    > **فَلَمَّا أَسْلَمَا وَتَلَّهُ لِلْجَبِينِ ۝ وَنَادَيْنَاهُ أَن يَا إِبْرَاهِيمُ**

    For the Kūfīs, **وَنَادَيْنَاهُ** is the jawāb and means simply **نَادَيْنَاهُ**. How the Baṣrīs explain it was set as homework; the teacher's guess was an omitted jawāb.

!!! example "al-Zumar 73 vs 71"
    > **حَتَّىٰ إِذَا جَاءُوهَا وَفُتِحَتْ أَبْوَابُهَا** (73, of Jannah)
    >
    > **حَتَّىٰ إِذَا جَاءُوهَا فُتِحَتْ أَبْوَابُهَا** (71, of Jahannam)

    Kūfīs read the wāw of āyah 73 as extra, so **فُتِحَتْ** is the jawāb. Baṣrīs read it as [**وَاو الحَال**](hal-circumstantial.md), *while its gates stand open*, and the jawāb is omitted.

    The ḥāl reading gives a striking contrast, which the teacher preferred: the doors of the Fire are shut and opened for a new arrival, like a **jail**; the doors of Jannah are **already flung open**, like a house welcoming guests.
